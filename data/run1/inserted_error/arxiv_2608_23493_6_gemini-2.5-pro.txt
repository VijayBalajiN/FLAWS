:explanation:
The introduced error is a fundamental mischaracterization of the properties of reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which is the core optimization objective of the proposed SRPO method.

The modified text incorrectly claims that the "mode-seeking" nature of reverse KL (`KL(student || teacher)`) leads to increased exploration and diversity of reasoning paths. Specifically, the modified conclusion states that this objective "effectively expands the policy's support to novel, correct reasoning paths."

This is conceptually wrong. In machine learning and information theory, reverse KL is known to be "mode-seeking" precisely because it encourages the student policy to find and place all of its probability mass on a single, high-probability mode of the teacher distribution. This behavior typically leads to a *reduction* in entropy and diversity, as the policy learns to commit to one good solution rather than exploring many. The objective that encourages covering multiple modes and thus increases diversity is the *forward* KL divergence (`KL(teacher || student)`), which is explicitly ablated in the paper as being inferior.

The error is subtle because it correctly uses the term "mode-seeking" but draws the opposite conclusion about its effect. It frames a mechanism for convergence and exploitation as a mechanism for exploration and diversity, directly undermining the paper's central claim about increasing the variety of reasoning paths. An expert would recognize this as a critical theoretical oversight.

:claim:
The SRPO training process increases the diversity of correct reasoning paths explored by the model. Compared to distillation from a larger external teacher, SRPO leads to a higher number of unique solution paths (4.1 vs. 2.3 per problem) for AIME'24.
