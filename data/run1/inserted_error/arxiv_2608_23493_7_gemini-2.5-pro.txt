The introduced error is a classic methodological flaw: a **confounded ablation study**. The paper's claim is that its "reset-with-memory" mechanism is critical for performance, and it uses an ablation study comparing this mechanism to an iterative approach to prove it.

The modifications introduce an unacknowledged difference between the main method and the ablated baseline:
1.  The main SRPO method is framed as a novel **Reinforcement Learning** algorithm that uses a PPO-style policy gradient objective to optimize a dense, reflection-guided reward.
2.  The "no state reset (iterative)" baseline is now explicitly described as being trained with **Behavior Cloning** (i.e., Supervised Fine-Tuning) on corrected trajectories.

This creates a confound. The 7.0 percentage point performance drop observed in the ablation is no longer solely attributable to the "state reset" mechanism. It could be partially or even entirely due to the fact that a sophisticated RL algorithm (SRPO) is being compared against a simpler and often less effective SFT algorithm (Behavior Cloning). The authors, however, incorrectly conclude that the performance gap is evidence for the inherent superiority of the state-reset mechanism, overlooking the fundamental difference in the optimization algorithms used. This is a subtle but critical error in experimental design and conclusion-drawing, very common in real research.
