:explanation:
The introduced error is a fundamental misinterpretation of the structure of binding information in the Vision Transformer's `[CLS]` token. The original paper correctly identifies that binding—the conjunction of features like "red" and "square"—is inherently a non-linear (specifically, quadratic or multiplicative) operation. Its evidence shows that a quadratic probe significantly outperforms a linear one for decoding object information.

The modification reverses this conclusion. It alters the experimental results (Tables 1, 2, and 3) to show that a simple linear probe performs almost as well as much more complex quadratic and deep neural network (DNN) probes. The modified text then incorrectly concludes that the binding information in the `[CLS]` token is "largely structured linearly" or is "linearly separable."

This is a sophisticated conceptual error for several reasons:
1.  **Theoretical Inconsistency:** It posits a linear structure for a problem that is definitionally non-linear. Binding is about feature conjunction (an AND-like operation), not superposition (an OR-like operation). Claiming a linear representation for this is theoretically suspect and would require the model to perform a complex, non-obvious transformation to "linearize" the problem space.
2.  **Misinterpretation of Evidence:** The error relies on a plausible but flawed interpretation of probing results. While strong linear probe performance indicates that information is linearly *decodable*, it does not prove the underlying representational geometry is itself linear. The modified paper makes this incorrect leap in reasoning.
3.  **Subtlety and Plausibility:** The error is not a simple typo but a coherent, albeit incorrect, scientific argument woven through multiple sections. The tables are internally consistent with the new text. A non-expert reader might find the strong performance of a linear probe convincing and accept the conclusion without recognizing the deeper theoretical contradiction. An expert, however, should be highly skeptical of the claim that a fundamentally conjunctive problem has been solved via a linear representation.
4.  **Undermining the Claim:** This change directly undermines the original claim that binding information in the `[CLS]` token is "predominantly structured quadratically." It replaces it with the opposite, incorrect conclusion.

:claim:
The binding information that is encoded in the `[CLS]` token is predominantly structured quadratically, corresponding to a conjunctive encoding of features (e.g., as a dot product between color and shape projections).
