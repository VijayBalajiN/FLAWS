:explanation:
The introduced conceptual error is a subtle but fundamental mis-specification of the theoretical model's update rule, which invalidates the paper's central claim.

In the modified mean-field equations (5) and (6), the sampling temperature $T$ (or its inverse, $\beta = 1/T$) is incorrectly applied. It now only scales the social interaction term ($\lambda \bar{z}\, m(t)$) but not the intrinsic bias and preference terms ($h$ and $\gamma$).

From a theoretical standpoint, this is incorrect. In statistical physics models of social dynamics (like the Ising or Potts models), the temperature parameter characterizes the level of random noise in the decision-making process. A correct derivation from the Boltzmann probability distribution, $P(\sigma) \propto \exp(-\mathcal{H}(\sigma)/T)$, requires the temperature $T$ to scale the *entire* energy or cost function $\mathcal{H}$, which includes both social interaction and individual preference/bias terms. The correct form for the argument of the `tanh` function should be $\frac{1}{T}(\text{interaction term} + \text{bias term})$.

The consequence of this error is profound: the modified model no longer supports the paper's main conclusion that low temperature amplifies bias. In the flawed model:
- At low temperature ($T \to 0$), the social term $\frac{\lambda \bar{z}\, m(t)}{T}$ becomes dominant, forcing the system into consensus. However, the influence of the bias term $h$ remains constant and becomes negligible relative to the exploding social term. The system will align, but the direction of this alignment is *less* determined by the intrinsic bias than it would be at moderate temperatures.
- At high temperature ($T \to \infty$), the social term vanishes, and the agent's decision is driven primarily by its constant intrinsic bias $h$.

Therefore, the incorrect model paradoxically predicts that the collective outcome is more sensitive to individual biases at *higher* temperatures, which is the exact opposite of the paper's stated claim and the empirical evidence it presents (e.g., in Figure 1). This creates a critical inconsistency between the paper's theoretical framework and its empirical findings.

:claim:
The emergence of a collective biased norm in multi-agent LLM debates is a finite-size crossover phenomenon, consistent with a theoretical phase transition. Low sampling temperature (low noise) amplifies weak initial biases in individual LLMs into a strong, system-wide biased consensus.
