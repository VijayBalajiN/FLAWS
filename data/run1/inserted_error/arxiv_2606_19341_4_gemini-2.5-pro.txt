The introduced conceptual error is a contradiction between the paper's central claim of "positive test-time scaling" and the detailed experimental results.

1.  **Misleading Interpretation:** The main text in the "Test-time Scaling Analysis" section now describes a "positive scaling trend" and cherry-picks the peak performance gain of +6.2% (at K=32). It omits mentioning that performance subsequently degrades. This is a common, realistic error where authors present data in the most favorable light, masking a potential weakness.

2.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The modified figure caption for Figure 3 explicitly states that performance peaks at K=32 and then *declines* at K=52. A careful reader who inspects the figure and its caption will notice this contradicts the oversimplified, "monotonic improvement" narrative presented in the abstract and the main analysis paragraph.

3.  **Theoretical Flaw:** This performance drop suggests a critical flaw in the agent's reasoning capability. Instead of "System-2 reasoning" that benefits from more time, the agent exhibits an "overthinking" or policy degradation problem. When given too large a reasoning budget, it may get stuck in loops, pursue irrelevant details, or fail to converge on a correct answer, leading to worse outcomes. This undermines the core claim that the model's performance robustly scales with computational budget.

4.  **Flawed Conclusion:** The original conclusion claims "consistent gains," which implies monotonic improvement. The modified version weakens this to "substantial gains," a subtle hedge that avoids making a verifiably false claim but still obscures the underlying issue of performance degradation.

In summary, the error is not a simple typo but a conceptual oversight where the authors overstate their contribution by ignoring contradictory evidence in their own results, a non-trivial flaw that requires cross-referencing the text, figure captions, and high-level claims to detect.
