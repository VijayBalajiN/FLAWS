:explanation:
The introduced error is a conceptual oversight where the paper's authors misinterpret their own empirical results to support their central claim, creating a contradiction that undermines the paper's main thesis.

The core claim is that OmniAgent "decouples the model's reasoning complexity and context cost from the raw duration of the video." This is supposedly achieved by purging raw media and only retaining a textual memory trace. However, the context cost depends on the length of this textual trace, which grows with each reasoning turn. The decoupling claim therefore hinges on the number of reasoning turns being independent of the video's duration.

The error is introduced in two steps:
1.  In Section 3 (Memory Consolidation), the claim is strengthened to explicitly state that the number of reasoning steps is determined by query complexity, not video duration, thus decoupling context cost.
2.  In Section 4.3 (Impact of Video Duration), the paper presents Table 4, which shows that the average number of turns *increases* from 8.5 to 12.5 (a ~47% increase) as video duration increases. The modified text deliberately misinterprets this significant increase as "nearly constant" and "negligible," and concludes that the "reasoning trace length is invariant to video duration."

This is a significant conceptual error. The authors draw a conclusion that is directly contradicted by their own data. They conflate a decrease in *sampling density* (turns per hour) with a lack of growth in *total turns*, thereby falsely "proving" their decoupling claim. An expert reader would recognize that a 47% increase in the length of the textual context is substantial and re-introduces a clear dependency between context cost and video duration, invalidating the paper's primary contribution. The mistake is subtle because the argument appears plausible on a surface level, especially given the distracting (but correct) observation about decreasing sampling density.
