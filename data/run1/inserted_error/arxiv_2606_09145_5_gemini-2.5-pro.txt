To regularize the latent space and enable sampling-based generation, we constrain the posterior as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z \mid c)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0, I)\big),
\end{equation}
where $q_\phi(z \mid c)$ is the stochastic latent distribution produced by the code encoder $E^c$, and the KL divergence encourages the learned posterior close to the standard normal prior.

:explanation:
The introduced conceptual error is a distributional mismatch between the latent space learned during training and the one used for generation, undermining the claim of high-fidelity synthesis.

During training, the model is optimized with two competing objectives for the code latent variable $z^c$:
1.  The VAE loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}}$) pushes the posterior $q(z|c)$ towards the standard normal prior $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. The modified text subtly misrepresents this objective's purpose as merely preventing overfitting, obscuring its critical role in aligning the aggregated posterior with the prior distribution to enable valid generative sampling.
2.  The alignment loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$) strongly pulls the code latent $z^c$ to be close to the corresponding prompt latent variable $z^p$.

The crucial oversight, made more salient by the modified text, is that there is no corresponding regularization forcing the distribution of prompt embeddings, $p(z^p)$, to match the standard normal prior. The prompt encoder learns an arbitrary distribution for $z^p$ that is shaped only by the contrastive task. Consequently, the alignment loss pulls the training distribution of code latents $z^c$ away from $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and towards this un-regularized prompt latent distribution.

During the generation stage, however, the method samples latent variables directly from the standard normal prior $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Due to the strong influence of the alignment loss during training, the prefix decoder $D$ was predominantly trained on latents from a distribution shaped by $z^p$, not $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Sampling from the standard normal prior thus constitutes sampling from what is likely a low-density region of the learned manifold, leading to out-of-distribution and low-fidelity code generation. This directly contradicts the paper's claim of producing a "high-fidelity distribution of synthetic code".

:claim:
The latent-conditioned synthesis of `PrivCode++` produces a more diverse and high-fidelity distribution of synthetic code compared to unconditional DP generation, as measured by t-SNE visualizations and quantitative fidelity metrics (e.g., CBERT-F1, AST-Sim).
