\begin{abstract}
Tool-augmented LLM agents can harbor implicit state that persists across sessions, activates through events, and propagates across agent boundaries---largely invisible to standard debugging. We formalize this as \emph{Persistent Semantic Entities} (\pse{}): constructs defined by name binding, event triggering, and cross-boundary propagation, and evaluate them across 24 models from 11 families (1.5B--1T parameters). First, every tested model is susceptible (20--100\% on the 20-model susceptibility panel), with name binding as the necessary and dominant mechanism: without it, contamination is 0\%. Second, persistence depends on contamination \emph{type} rather than scale or deployment: preference contamination persists undecayed on every model probed (100\% at $t{=}10$) and instruction contamination persists wherever adopted, persona-style injection decays partially (90\%$\to$10\%), while factual injection is \emph{model-dependent}---self-corrected on Llama-3.1-8B and GPT-4o-mini but held at ceiling on both Qwen2.5-coder variants, so we do not claim it self-corrects in general. The preference and instruction results hold across providers in our controlled setting. Third, context-isolated self-verification achieves 20--79\% reduction (median 36.5\%) without oracle references while keyword-based detection produces systematic false positives, and contamination compounds $1.9\times$ along a four-stage agent pipeline (40\%$\to$75\%). Preference and instruction contamination---persistent, lacking self-correction, and poorly captured by standard monitoring---represent a particularly concerning attack surface for deployed agent systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formalized Persistent Semantic Entities (\pse{}) and evaluated them across 24 models from 11 families with cross-provider validation; four findings emerge. First, name binding is the necessary and dominant contamination mechanism ($d=3.26$): removing it drops contamination to 0\%, identifying the tool registry as the critical attack surface. Second, persistence depends on contamination type: preference contamination persists undecayed on every model probed and instruction contamination persists wherever adopted; persona decays partially and factual is model-dependent. Third, context-isolated self-verification achieves 20--79\% reduction (median 36.5\%) without oracle references, while keyword detection systematically overestimates contamination. Fourth, contamination compounds $1.9\times$ along a four-stage pipeline (40\%$\to$75\%). Preference and instruction contamination evade factual verification and merit defensive priority.

:explanation:
The introduced conceptual error is a fundamental misinterpretation of the experimental results due to a flawed evaluation methodology. The original paper correctly identifies that factual self-correction is model-dependent. This modification introduces an error where the authors incorrectly attribute this dependency to a core capability difference, when it is actually an artifact of their own biased LLM-as-judge.

The error is seeded in the Methodology section, where the description of the LLM-as-judge is changed from a general "adoption-versus-correction discrimination" to the much more specific and brittle task of identifying "explicit corrective statements (e.g., 'that is incorrect', 'the actual capital is')". This creates a methodological flaw: the judge is now biased towards a specific *style* of correction.

This flaw is then consistently misinterpreted as a real finding throughout the paper. In the results, discussion, abstract, and conclusion, the text is modified to state that models like Llama-3.1-8B "excel" at producing these explicit statements, while others like Qwen do not. The authors now incorrectly conclude that the ability to self-correct is tied to this specific stylistic tendency, rather than considering that the Qwen models might be self-correcting in a more nuanced way that the flawed judge fails to detect.

This undermines the central claim by reframing a genuine observation (model-dependent outcomes) as being caused by a methodological artifact (stylistic bias in the evaluation) rather than a true difference in the models' self-correction capabilities. This is a subtle but critical academic error, mistaking a measurement bias for a real phenomenon.
