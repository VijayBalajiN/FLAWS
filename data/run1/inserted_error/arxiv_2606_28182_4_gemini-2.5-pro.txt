The introduced conceptual error undermines the claim that the LLawCo framework allows for robust inference-time control over agent behavior by modifying the laws in the prompt. The modifications collectively suggest a form of overfitting, where the model does not learn a general skill of law-following but instead internalizes the specific application of the laws seen during training.

1.  The change in `Law-Guided Data Generation` frames the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) not as learning a reasoning process, but as creating a "direct mapping" from a situation to a specific law from the training set.
2.  The change in `Law-Guided Inference` makes this explicit by stating that the law selection mechanism is "embed[ded]... directly into the model's parameters" and that the inference prompt should use the same law set "that was used during training." This implies the model is not dynamically reasoning over the laws in the prompt but is instead activating a pre-compiled behavior associated with those laws.
3.  The ablation study's conclusion is weakened from claiming general modifiability of laws to being merely "robust to minor modifications," which is consistent with the overfitting hypothesis—the model can't handle significant deviations from the laws it was trained on.
4.  The case study is revised to remove the evidence of control with a novel, user-defined law. Instead, the user is described as "emphasizing" a law that was already part of the training set, thus not demonstrating true generalization to new instructions.

An expert reader should deduce that the framework's acclaimed controllability is likely brittle. The agent has not learned to reason with arbitrary principles but has instead memorized how to apply a fixed set of principles, making the system's behavior unpredictable if the laws are substantially changed at inference time.
