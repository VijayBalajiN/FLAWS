:explanation:
The introduced conceptual error is in the core mechanism of the Conflict Perception Training (CPT). The modified text provides a plausible but flawed justification for how the two conflicting representations, $\mathbf{z}_1$ and $\mathbf{z}_2$, are generated before being pushed apart by the contrastive loss $\mathcal{L}_{cacl}$.

Specifically, the setup is now explicitly designed to compare a visually-grounded feature (if the conflict source is the image) with a pure text feature (if the source is text or world knowledge). The justification, "to ensure the representation remains true to its non-visual origin," sounds reasonable but hides a fundamental flaw in representation learning.

The theoretical oversight is that effective contrastive learning for separating semantic *concepts* requires comparing their representations within a unified, shared embedding space. By design, this modified method compares features from different domains (an "apples and oranges" comparison). This gives the model a trivial shortcut: instead of learning the complex, semantic conflict between concepts (e.g., "U.S. President" vs. "Football Award"), it can simply learn to separate features based on their *modality of origin*. The loss function can be minimized by simply learning that visually-grounded representations are different from text-based representations, a much easier but useless task that fails to capture the intended conceptual conflict. A correct approach would project both conflict factors into a common semantic space before applying the contrastive loss. This error undermines the central claim that the training on CAC effectively teaches the model to perceive multimodal conflicts, as it's actually learning a simpler, modality-based artifact.

:claim:
Training a model on the newly created Conflict Attribution Corpus (CAC), which contains fine-grained annotations of conflict factors and their sources (image, text, or world knowledge), is necessary and effective for teaching the model to perceive multimodal conflicts.
