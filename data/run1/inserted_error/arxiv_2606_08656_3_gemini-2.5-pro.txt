:explanation:
The core conceptual error introduced is the claim that a long-horizon, cumulative reward signal is more effective for training the memory-update policy than a one-step (next-game) proxy reward.

This is a plausible but theoretically flawed argument in the context of high-variance, partially observable games like poker. Here's why it's an error:
1.  **High Variance and Credit Assignment:** In poker, the outcome of future hands depends heavily on stochastic events (i.e., the cards dealt). A cumulative reward signal sums rewards over these future, noisy events. This makes it extremely difficult to determine if a good outcome was due to a high-quality memory update or simply a lucky sequence of future hands. This high variance destabilizes training and makes credit assignment unreliable.
2.  **Myopia vs. Stability:** The modified paper argues that a one-step reward is "myopic." While it is myopic, this is often a necessary trade-off for stability. The one-step reward provides a much cleaner, lower-variance signal that directly links a memory update ($m_t$) to its immediate consequence (the outcome of game $t+1$), which is the standard and more robust approach in such settings.
3.  **Inconsistent with Best Practices:** The introduced error runs counter to established knowledge in reinforcement learning, where techniques like TD-learning and actor-critic methods are designed specifically to reduce the variance of long-horizon returns. The original paper's use of a one-step proxy is consistent with these principles.

The modifications systematically alter the methodology, figure captions, experimental results (by falsifying the ablation study in Table 9), and conclusion to consistently support this incorrect claim, creating a coherent but flawed scientific narrative that a graduate-level researcher in the field should be able to identify as suspect.

:claim:
For training the memory-update policy, using a one-step proxy reward (i.e., the outcome of the very next game) is more effective and stable than using a cumulative reward signal summed over all future games in the episode.
