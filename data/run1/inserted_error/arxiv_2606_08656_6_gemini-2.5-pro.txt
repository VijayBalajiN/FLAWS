:explanation:
The introduced conceptual error is the **misapplication of long-horizon cumulative rewards for credit assignment in a setting that requires rapid, online adaptation.**

1.  **Theoretical Flaw:** The modified paper now incorrectly argues that using a cumulative return (sum of all future rewards) is superior for training the memory update policy. In highly stochastic environments like poker, where future outcomes depend heavily on random events (e.g., card deals), this approach introduces enormous variance into the training signal. A good memory update can be unfairly penalized by a subsequent string of bad luck, and a poor update can be reinforced by good luck, making it extremely difficult for the model to learn a reliable adaptation strategy. The correct approach for this problem, as used in the original paper, is a low-variance, one-step (next-game) reward that provides a much cleaner signal for credit assignment.

2.  **Undermining the Claim:** The claim focuses on the ability to adapt to a *sudden opponent change*. The introduced flaw directly cripples this capability. An agent trained with a noisy, long-horizon reward signal will learn very slowly and struggle to distinguish between a change in opponent strategy and random environmental noise. When the opponent switches, the agent's memory update is punished or rewarded based on a signal that is diluted by multiple future games, preventing it from quickly identifying and reacting to the new opponent's patterns.

3.  **Plausibility and Subtlety:** The error is plausible because the justification—"encouraging far-sighted memory updates"—sounds theoretically sound in a general sense. The error's subtlety is reinforced by an internally consistent, but incorrect, narrative. The methodology section now advocates for the flawed approach, the reward design ablation (Table 5) is falsified to "prove" its superiority, and the cross-opponent evaluation (Table 4) unwittingly demonstrates the real-world consequence of the flaw: poor adaptation. However, the text misinterprets this failure not as a self-inflicted wound from a poor reward design, but as a general, unsolved challenge for the field, making the paper appear self-critical while being fundamentally misguided.
