:explanation:
The introduced error is a critical conceptual oversight regarding the robustness of the "pseudo-max" approximation, which is central to the paper's top-$\delta$ selection strategy.

1.  **Ambiguity in the Approximation Method**: In Section 3.2, the precise definition of the pseudo-max `\tilde{m}_i` as a strict maximum over a subset of tokens (which guarantees `\tilde{m}_i <= m_i`) is removed. It is replaced with a vague description of an "estimation" that is "derived" from these subsets. This introduces the possibility that the estimation method is not a strict lower bound and could potentially *overestimate* the true maximum attention score `m_i`.

2.  **Flawed Robustness Analysis**: In Section 5.2, the original text correctly identified that `\tilde{m}_i` is a lower bound on `m_i`, making the approximation a "fail-safe" mechanism that might keep more tokens but never incorrectly discard important ones. The modified text completely removes this crucial guarantee. Instead, it exclusively analyzes the case of underestimation (`\tilde{m}_i < m_i`), framing it as the "primary source of approximation error" and concluding that it only leads to a minor efficiency penalty.

**The core error**: The modified analysis creates a critical blind spot by failing to consider or even acknowledge the possibility of overestimation (`\tilde{m}_i > m_i`). If the ambiguous estimation method from Section 3.2 were to overestimate the max score, the filtering threshold `\tilde{m}_i - \delta` would be raised too high. This would cause the FFD algorithm to incorrectly prune tokens that are actually important, leading to a potential catastrophic loss of accuracy. The paper's claim of maintaining downstream performance is thus fundamentally undermined, as its core selection mechanism contains an unanalyzed failure mode that could compromise model quality. This is a subtle but significant theoretical flaw that a domain expert would identify as a major gap in the method's justification.
