The introduced error undermines the paper's central claim against learnable positional embedding components by making its own proposed method, nD-RoPE, susceptible to the same failure mode it criticizes.

The original paper argues that learnable methods like RoPE-Mixed suffer from "frequency collapse" because optimization can lead to unstable, anisotropic frequency distributions. It presents nD-RoPE's fixed, regular-simplex geometry as a robust, deterministic solution that "guarantees" isotropy.

The modification replaces the "random" head-wise rotation of the simplex wave vectors with a "learnable" one. This is a subtle but critical conceptual flaw. By making the rotation matrix $Q_h$ a trainable parameter for each attention head, the orientation of the frequency basis is no longer fixed but is subject to optimization. This re-introduces the very problem the paper critiques: the optimization process could drive the learned rotations to a degenerate state. For example, the model might learn to align the simplexes for multiple heads in a similar, non-isotropic direction to minimize training loss, causing a "directional collapse."

This change invalidates the claim that the method "guarantees" isotropy, as the final geometric configuration is now data-dependent and prone to the same optimization instabilities as other learnable methods. The core theoretical advantage of nD-RoPE over RoPE-Mixed is therefore compromised, significantly weakening the paper's key claim.
