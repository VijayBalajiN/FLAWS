The introduced error is a subtle but significant misinterpretation of the ablation study results, creating a direct contradiction between the paper's textual claims and its own data in Table 4.

The modified text asserts that the comprehensive context from specialized research agents is "critical for good calibration" and that removing it "increases the model's calibration error significantly." This claim supports the paper's narrative about the value of its complex context-gathering system.

However, the data in Table 4 shows the opposite:
- **Full System (Row 1):** The calibration error (`Cal_B`) is 0.039.
- **Deal Card only (Row 7):** The calibration error (`Cal_B`) is 0.036.

The data indicates that limiting context to the "Deal Card" actually *improves* the model's calibration slightly (0.036 is a better score than 0.039). The overall performance degradation (worse Brier score) is entirely due to a collapse in *discrimination* (`Disc_B` worsens from 0.112 to 0.137), meaning the model with less context is worse at distinguishing high-risk deals from low-risk ones.

This conceptual error is plausible because an author might misinterpret the components of the Brier score or fail to scrutinize the decomposition, instead attributing the overall performance gain to the most intuitive explanation (better information leads to better-calibrated probabilities). This mistake fundamentally misrepresents *why* the proposed system is effective, thereby undermining the key claim. The error is reinforced across two sections to create a coherent but false narrative.
