:original_text:
We presented \method{}, a post-training method that converts episode-level outcomes into hindsight-guided dense, reflection-augmented supervision, overcoming the inefficiency of sparse-reward RL. The mechanism is best understood not as self-distillation but as on-policy RL with reflection-guided exploration: reflection-conditioned teachers steer the student into high-reward regions of its \emph{own} policy space 
 rather than imitating a distribution beyond its representational capacity. Across 10 benchmarks spanning math, code, agent, science, and logic, \method{} yields reproducible gains (multi-seed bootstrap CIs, $p<0.005$), matching or exceeding larger-teacher distillation while requiring $\sim$$3.8\times$ fewer total FLOPs than GRPO and naturally mitigating catastrophic forgetting under continual learning. Moving forward, we aim to refine reflection quality via external verification or better calibration, and to extend the framework beyond verifiable-outcome tasks, including multimodal reasoning and longer-horizon tool-use trajectories.

:modified_text:
We presented \method{}, a post-training method that converts episode-level outcomes into hindsight-guided dense, reflection-augmented supervision, overcoming the inefficiency of sparse-reward RL. The mechanism is best understood not as simple self-distillation, but as a form of \emph{representational annealing}. The dense, per-token rewards from the reflection-augmented teacher provide a strong, low-variance training signal that encourages a more compressed and efficient internal representation of the task. This compression frees latent capacity, allowing the policy to maintain higher entropy over valid reasoning paths rather than collapsing to a single mode, as is common in sparse-reward RL. Across 10 benchmarks spanning math, code, agent, science, and logic, \method{} yields reproducible gains (multi-seed bootstrap CIs, $p<0.005$), matching or exceeding larger-teacher distillation while requiring $\sim$$3.8\times$ fewer total FLOPs than GRPO and naturally mitigating catastrophic forgetting under continual learning. Moving forward, we aim to refine reflection quality via external verification or better calibration, and to extend the framework beyond verifiable-outcome tasks, including multimodal reasoning and longer-horizon tool-use trajectories.

:explanation:
The conceptual error introduced is a fabricated and theoretically unsound mechanism called "representational annealing" to explain the effectiveness of the SRPO method.

This explanation is flawed for several key reasons:
1.  **Contradiction with the Core Algorithm**: The paper's methodology (Section 3.2.2) correctly states that it uses a reverse KL-divergence objective. Reverse KL is fundamentally "mode-seeking," meaning it encourages the student policy to find and concentrate its probability mass on a single, high-probability mode of the teacher's distribution. This process inherently reduces policy entropy and diversity. The modified text incorrectly claims the mechanism allows the policy to "maintain higher entropy over valid reasoning paths," which is the opposite of what a reverse-KL objective promotes.
2.  **Fabricated Concept**: "Representational annealing" is a plausible-sounding but non-existent term in machine learning literature. The explanation that a strong training signal "frees latent capacity" which in turn increases policy entropy is a hand-wavy, "just-so" story with no grounding in optimization theory. A strong, focused signal is more likely to cause representational collapse around the target behavior, not expansion.
3.  **Plausible but Incorrect Rationale**: The error is designed to be subtle because it provides a convenient, albeit false, explanation for the paper's empirical results, particularly the claim of increased solution diversity in Appendix B.5 and the improved inference-time refinement capability. It creates an internally consistent narrative (higher entropy -> more paths to explore -> better refinement) that is, however, disconnected from and contradictory to the actual mathematical formulation of the algorithm.

An expert should detect that the authors are misinterpreting the effect of their own algorithm, inventing a flawed theory to fit their observations, which is a classic and significant conceptual oversight in academic research.
