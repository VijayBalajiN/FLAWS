:explanation:
The error introduced is a subtle but critical reversal of the arguments in the KL divergence used for the Omni-Perception Loss. The original, correct formulation $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t \| p_t^m)$ (forward KL) would incentivize the policy under full input ($p_t$) to be confident about tokens that the policy under masked input ($p_t^m$) is uncertain about, correctly promoting faithfulness.

The modified formulation uses $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t^m \| p_t)$ (reverse KL). Maximizing this objective has a very different, and detrimental, theoretical effect. Reverse KL is "zero-avoiding" or "mode-covering" for its second argument ($p_t$). To maximize it, the model is incentivized to make the full-input distribution $p_t$ very broad and uncertain to avoid assigning low probability to any token the masked model $p_t^m$ might generate. This would lead to less confident and less precise reasoning under the full input, directly undermining the goal of the Omni-Perception Reward, which is to generate specific, high-quality cues.

This error breaks the central "synergy" claim. Instead of creating a better optimization landscape for the reward, the flawed loss formulation creates a worse one by encouraging diffuse, uncertain outputs. The error is made more subtle because the textual explanation of the loss still describes the goal of the *correct* formulation, and the theoretical analysis in the appendix proceeds with a proof that is only valid for the *correct* (forward) KL divergence, creating a sharp contradiction between the formula presented and its supposed theoretical justification.

:claim:
There is a synergistic relationship between the Omni-Perception Reward and the Omni-Perception Loss; the loss improves faithfulness, which in turn creates a more effective optimization landscape for the reward to improve cue utilization, leading to combined performance gains that are greater than applying either component in isolation.
