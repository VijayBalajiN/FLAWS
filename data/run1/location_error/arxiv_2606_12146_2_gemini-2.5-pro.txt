\section{Construction of Symmetric Wave Vectors}
\label{appendix:gridpe_cal}
This section details a direct construction for a set of $n+1$ symmetric, non-axis-aligned wave vectors in an $n$-dimensional space. The goal is to produce a set of vectors $\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\}_{i=1}^{n+1}$ that span the space and satisfy the zero-centroid condition $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_i = 0$, ensuring no net directional bias.

The construction begins by selecting the $n$ standard orthogonal basis vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Let $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \dots, \mathbf{e}_{n}$ be these basis vectors, where $\mathbf{e}_i$ has a 1 in the $i$-th coordinate and 0s elsewhere. These first $n$ vectors are defined as:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_i = \mathbf{e}_i, \qquad \text{for } i=1, \dots, n.
\end{equation}
These vectors clearly span $\mathbb{R}^n$, but they are axis-aligned and do not sum to zero. To satisfy the zero-centroid condition and introduce cross-dimensional coupling, we define the $(n+1)$-th vector as the negative sum of the first $n$ vectors:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n+1} = - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \boldsymbol{\omega}_i = - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{e}_i.
\end{equation}
This last vector $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n+1}$ is a vector of all -1s, i.e., $(-1, -1, \dots, -1)$.

The complete set of $n+1$ wave vectors is therefore:
\begin{equation}
\{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \dots, \mathbf{e}_n, -\mathbf{1}\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}$ is the vector of all ones. By construction, this set of vectors sums to zero:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \boldsymbol{\omega}_i = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{e}_i\right) + \left(-\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{e}_i\right) = \mathbf{0}.
\end{equation}
The presence of the vector $-\mathbf{1}$ ensures that the wave-vector set is not separable along the coordinate axes. For example, in 2D, the vectors are $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(-1,-1)$. The interaction of these three directions forces the model to learn representations that are sensitive to diagonal and off-diagonal relationships.

This simple and deterministic construction yields a set of $n+1$ wave vectors that span $\mathbb{R}^n$ and are balanced around the origin. The resulting wave-vector matrix $\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{(n)} \in \mathbb{R}^{(n+1) \times n}$ is formed by stacking these vectors $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i^\top$ as rows. This provides an explicit $n$-dimensional realization suitable for constructing non-axis-aligned and stable positional encodings.
