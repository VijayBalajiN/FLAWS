The identified errors represent critical flaws that question the paper's scientific validity and integrity.

1.  **Fabricated Publication and Citation Details:** The most severe error is the use of impossible publication details. The paper claims to be published in "PMLR 306, 2026," a future proceeding with a future copyright date. Furthermore, it cites references with full publication details for conferences that have not yet occurred, such as "ICML’25." This is a violation of academic standards, as one cannot cite the published proceedings of a future event. This suggests the paper and its references may be fabricated, which is grounds for immediate rejection.

2.  **Unfair Experimental Comparison:** The paper claims to make a "fair comparison" for resolution extrapolation by integrating YaRN. However, the results in Table 2 (Kinetics-400) reveal a fundamental flaw in this methodology. The in-domain (224x224) accuracy for the `RoPE-Axial` baseline jumps from 73.23% to 75.03% when YaRN is added. YaRN is an extrapolation technique and should not improve in-domain performance. This large discrepancy indicates that the "with YaRN" and "without YaRN" experiments were not run under comparable conditions, invalidating the central claims about superior extrapolation performance.

3.  **Misleading Interpretation of Experimental Results:** The authors mischaracterize their own findings regarding computational cost and performance. They claim that switching to dot-product attention with nD-RoPE results in a "slight performance drop". However, their own results in Table 4 show a substantial drop in mIoU from 71.91% to 67.12%. Additionally, they present the computational savings from this switch as a benefit of nD-RoPE, which is misleading. The choice of attention mechanism is orthogonal to the positional encoding, and a direct comparison (Table 8, Vector vs. Vector attention) shows nD-RoPE slightly *increases* computational cost.
