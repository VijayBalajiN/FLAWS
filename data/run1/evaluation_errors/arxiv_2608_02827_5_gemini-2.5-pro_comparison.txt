The paper's most serious flaw is its reliance on numerous citations to papers supposedly published in 2025 and 2026, with the venue itself dated 2026. A scientific work cannot be based on, or place itself in the context of, research that does not yet exist. This undermines the entire scholarly foundation of the paper, making its claims impossible to verify against the cited literature and constituting a fundamental violation of academic practice.

A second major error is a critical inconsistency in the experimental methodology for the "real-world" tasks. The initial phenomenon is demonstrated using a multi-round debate structure, but the subsequent intervention experiments are conducted using "one-step conversational memory." This change of protocol between demonstration and intervention invalidates the claim that the theoretically-motivated intervention is effective for the originally observed phenomenon.

Third, the evaluation of "performance" for the investment task is unreliable. It is based on market-adjusted returns over an extremely short, ~5-week period. Financial market performance over such a brief window is dominated by noise and volatility, making it an unsuitable measure for the quality of an investment strategy. Conclusions drawn from this metric are not credible.

Finally, the validation of the theoretical model is weakened by conflating the externally controlled experimental variable (logit bias `b`) with the theoretical parameter (`h_bias`). The paper acknowledges that the total bias depends on both the controlled `b` and the LLM's inherent bias, yet the phase diagrams used for validation are plotted against `b`.
