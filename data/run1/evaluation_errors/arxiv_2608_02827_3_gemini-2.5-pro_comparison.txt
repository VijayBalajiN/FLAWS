The most serious error is a factual impossibility in the experimental methodology. The paper claims to evaluate investment performance on a future time window (December 2025 – January 2026). As the paper was submitted for a 2026 conference, this evaluation could not have been performed. This is likely a major typographical error in the dates, but as written, it describes an impossible experiment, severely damaging the credibility of the real-world task results.

The second error concerns the reporting and experimental design for the key intervention result in Figure 7. The paper describes creating a single heterogeneous agent ensemble with a *fixed set* of different sampling temperatures. However, the corresponding plot shows the performance of this heterogeneous mixture as a continuous curve that varies with "Temperature T" on the x-axis. It is not explained how the outcome of a fixed mixture can be a function of a single, varying temperature, making the plot illogical and the results uninterpretable.

The third error is a significant inconsistency in the experimental protocol between the problem demonstration and the proposed solution. The emergent biased consensus is initially demonstrated in Section 2 using a debate with multi-round conversational memory. However, when the paper presents its main intervention (agent heterogeneity) in Section 6.2, the protocol is changed to use only one-step memory. This change means the proposed solution is validated on a simplified version of the problem, and it is not demonstrated to be effective for the original, more complex setting, weakening the practical claims of the paper.
