The paper's central claim—that SRPO is an on-policy algorithm that avoids the distribution shift inherent in off-policy methods—is contradicted by its own methodology.

The paper makes two mutually exclusive claims about its core training procedure:
1.  It claims to be strictly on-policy by using student-generated rollouts that are then scored by the teacher, explicitly stating it does *not* use teacher-generated trajectories for training.
2.  However, the detailed description of the "On-Policy Sampling" step in Section 3.2.2 and the pseudocode in Algorithm 1 state the exact opposite: that trajectories are sampled from the *teacher* policy ($\pi_T$) to train the student.

Sampling from a teacher policy to train a student policy is, by definition, off-policy distillation. This fundamental contradiction invalidates the paper's claims about avoiding exposure bias and distribution shift through on-policy learning. The flawed reasoning is further exemplified by the assertion that sharing parameters makes the sampling on-policy, which is incorrect as the conditioning context is different, leading to different distributions.

This fatal flaw is compounded by the ablation study (Table 1), which presents results for an "Off-policy (teacher trajectories)" baseline. This is nonsensical, as the main method described in the text and algorithm is already using teacher trajectories, making the ablation experiment's purpose and results highly questionable.
