The paper's central utility claims are invalidated by a serious methodological flaw: tuning hyper-parameters on the final test sets. The caption of Table 4 explicitly states that the evaluation for different latent variable dimensions ($d_z$) was performed on the test benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, and EvalPlus). The text in Section 5.5 confirms this by selecting the best-performing dimension ($d_z=768$) based on these test scores.

This practice leads to an optimistic bias in the reported results, as the hyper-parameters are overfitted to the specific test data. Consequently, the paper's claims that PrivCode++ "achieves substantially higher utility than baselines" and is "competitive with relaxed-privacy baselines" are not reliably supported, as the performance of PrivCode++ is likely inflated, making the comparison unfair. The correct experimental procedure would be to tune hyper-parameters on a separate validation set, not the final test set.
