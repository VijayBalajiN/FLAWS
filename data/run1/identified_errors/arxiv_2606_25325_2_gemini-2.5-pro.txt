\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.} Proposition~\ref{proposition1} establishes $\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{omni}}$ as a variational upper bound on the conditional mutual information. While maximizing this bound could theoretically increase the variational gap $\Delta_{\mathrm{var}}$ rather than the mutual information $I_\theta$, our framework prevents this trivial solution through parameter sharing. Since the variational approximation $\pi_\theta(\cdot | X^{\tilde{m}})$ and the full policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | X)$ share the same backbone, they are structurally coupled. Arbitrarily inflating $\Delta_{\mathrm{var}}$ would require distorting the masked policy, which would inadvertently degrade the full policy. Consequently, maximizing $\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{omni}}$ forces the model to genuinely increase the true mutual information, ensuring faithful modality-specific perception.

Conversely, if the distribution remains similar after masking $m$, the model is likely relying on spurious cross-modal correlations to hallucinate content about the missing modality.

:explanation:
There is a fundamental contradiction between the stated goal of the Omni-Perception Loss and its mathematical formulation, rendering the method invalid.

The paper claims that to enforce faithfulness, the model's output distribution for modality-specific tokens should change substantially when the corresponding modality is masked. This is described as encouraging a "large KL divergence". The theoretical analysis in the Appendix further reinforces this, stating that the objective is to be "maximizing $\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{omni}}$".

However, the final objective function, $\mathcal{J}_\mathsf{total}(\theta) = \mathcal{J}_{\mathsf{GRPO}}(\theta) + \beta \mathcal{J}_\mathsf{omni}(\theta)$, is minimized during training. Since $\mathcal{J}_\mathsf{omni}(\theta)$ is a sum of non-negative KL-divergence terms and $\beta > 0$, the optimization process actively *minimizes* the KL divergence. This forces the output distributions under full and masked inputs to become as similar as possible.

This implementation achieves the exact opposite of the stated goal. Instead of suppressing cross-modal hallucination, it explicitly trains the model to generate the same modality-specific evidence tokens even when the corresponding modality is absent, thereby encouraging the very behavior it was designed to prevent. This error invalidates the core technical contribution of the Omni-Perception Loss.
