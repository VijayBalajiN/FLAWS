:explanation:
The paper describes a training process that is technically contradictory and impossible to implement as stated. In Section 3.2, it claims that the junior model $\mathcal{M}_J$ is used in a "frozen forward pass" to generate hidden state representations ($h^c$ and $h^p$) from code and prompts. A "frozen" model implies that its weights are not updated and gradients are not computed for it during backpropagation.

However, in the same training loop (Algorithm 1, line 8), the paper states that the parameters of this same junior model $\mathcal{M}_J$ are updated based on the cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$. This loss depends on the prefix embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prefix}}$, which is a direct function of the hidden state $h^c$ produced by $\mathcal{M}_J$. For the gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ to fully update $\mathcal{M}_J$, they would need to backpropagate through $\mathcal{M}_J$ in both its role as a generator and its role as the feature extractor that produced $h^c$.

A model cannot be simultaneously "frozen" (blocking gradients) and have its weights updated via backpropagation through the same computational path. This fundamental contradiction makes the core training algorithm of PrivCode++ ill-defined, undermining the validity and replicability of the entire method.
