The paper's core claim that its "activation-level correction" is superior to standard "isolation-based" mitigation is critically undermined by a severe inconsistency in the experimental results, suggesting potential data fabrication.

An ablation study in Table 3 reports results for `AcMAS` with its correction mechanism replaced by agent isolation ("w/o Correction"). For the HotPotQA dataset, this experiment reports a Task Completion Rate (TCR) of 0.68 and an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 0.15.

Critically, these are the exact same TCR and ASR values reported for the `G-Safeguard` baseline in Table 2 for the HotPotQA dataset. This is highly improbable because `AcMAS` and `G-Safeguard` are reported to have vastly different detection capabilities. As shown in Table 1 for the same dataset, `AcMAS` achieves an F1 score of 0.94, while `G-Safeguard` only achieves 0.70.

Since the two methods have different detection accuracies, they would isolate different sets of agents. This should lead to different task-level outcomes (TCR and ASR). The fact that the results are identical strongly suggests that the results for the "w/o Correction" ablation were not experimentally derived but were instead copied from the baseline, invalidating the entire ablation study and the evidence supporting one of the paper's main contributions.
