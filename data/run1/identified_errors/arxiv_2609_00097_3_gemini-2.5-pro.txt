A core theoretical pillar of FFD is the robustness of the Pseudo-Max approximation. By computing $\tilde{m}_i$ over a high-confidence subset (sinks and local context), we obtain a value that is, by definition, a lower bound on the true global maximum score, i.e., $\tilde{m}_i \le m_i = \max_j s_{ij}$. This property provides a mathematically rigorous guarantee against false negatives.

To formalize this, recall the ideal retention criterion from Eq.~\ref{eq:threshold}: a token $j$ should be kept if $s_{ij} \ge m_i - \delta$. Our practical filter uses the condition $s_{ij} \ge \tilde{m}_i - \delta$. Since $\tilde{m}_i \le m_i$, it directly follows that $\tilde{m}_i - \delta \le m_i - \delta$. This means our filtering threshold is always more permissive than or equal to the ideal threshold. 

Therefore, any token $j$ that satisfies the ideal condition ($s_{ij} \ge m_i - \delta$) is \textit{guaranteed} to be satisfied by our practical condition ($s_{ij} \ge \tilde{m}_i - \delta$), because $s_{ij} \ge m_i - \delta \ge \tilde{m}_i - \delta$. This demonstrates that the Pseudo-Max approximation is a fail-safe mechanism.

:explanation:
The paper's central claim of being a "fail-safe mechanism" with a "mathematically rigorous guarantee against false negatives" is based on the assertion that the pseudo-max score, $\tilde{m}_i$, is a strict lower bound of the true maximum score, $m_i$ (i.e., $\tilde{m}_i \leq m_i$). This assertion is fundamentally flawed.

The true maximum score, $m_i$, is defined as the maximum of pre-softmax attention scores calculated using the full-precision key vectors over the entire context. However, the pseudo-max, $\tilde{m}_i$, is calculated using low-bit *quantized* key vectors over a *subset* of the context (sink and local tokens), as specified in Algorithm 1.

Due to quantization noise, the approximate scores used to compute $\tilde{m}_i$ can be either higher or lower than the true scores. It is entirely possible for the quantization error to be large and positive for a token within the subset used for $\tilde{m}_i$, while being negative for the token that has the true global maximum score. In such a scenario, the computed pseudo-max $\tilde{m}_i$ could be greater than the true global max $m_i$.

If $\tilde{m}_i > m_i$, the selection threshold $\tilde{m}_i - \delta$ becomes stricter than the ideal threshold $m_i - \delta$. This invalidates the core guarantee, as it could lead to the erroneous pruning of important tokens (false negatives), directly contradicting the "fail-safe" property claimed throughout the paper. The entire theoretical justification for the method's safety, including the formal error analysis in Appendix B, rests on this incorrect assumption.
