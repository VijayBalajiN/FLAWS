Proposition A.6 incorrectly claims that the condition for market unraveling is a necessary consequence of the model's setup. Specifically, it states, "Because the assumption $q_H > c_H$ keeps HTL-intensive production viable under full-information pricing, we have $\bar{p}_t < c_H \leq q_H$".

This is a non-sequitur. The pooled price, $\bar{p}_t = \lambda_t q_H + (1-\lambda_t) q_L$, is a weighted average of the high and low quality values. The assumption $q_H > c_H$ ensures that high-cost producers are profitable if their quality is known. However, it does not imply that the pooled price $\bar{p}_t$ must be less than the high cost $c_H$. In fact, if the share of high-cost producers ($\lambda_t$) is sufficiently high, the pooled price $\bar{p}_t$ will be greater than $c_H$.

The condition $\bar{p}_t < c_H$ is the contingent *trigger* that causes high-cost producers to exit and initiates the value collapse feedback loop. By presenting this trigger as an unconditional fact that is always true, the proposition misrepresents the core dynamic. This fundamental error in reasoning suggests a misunderstanding of the adverse selection mechanism and undermines the entire formal justification for the paper's main claim.
