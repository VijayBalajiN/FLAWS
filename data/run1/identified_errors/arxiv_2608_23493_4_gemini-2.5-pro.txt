The paper's central claim of being 3.8x more computationally efficient than GRPO is undermined by a severe contradiction in its FLOPs accounting. The total FLOPs count for SRPO explicitly includes a "rethinking rollout" which accounts for over 22% of the total cost. However, the appendix states this rollout is not used for training and its cost could be removed without changing the objective. Furthermore, the text makes a conflicting claim that this same component is "essentially free" due to an implementation detail. A major cost component cannot simultaneously be essential to the reported total, optional for the training algorithm, and computationally free. This inconsistency makes the core efficiency claim unreliable.

A second major flaw is that the conclusion fundamentally mischaracterizes the proposed method. It claims the mechanism is "not as self-distillation but as on-policy RL". This contradicts the methodology section, where the training objective is explicitly defined as minimizing the reverse KL-divergence to a teacher's distribution, which is, by definition, a form of on-policy distillation. Misrepresenting the core mechanism of the work is a serious conceptual error.
