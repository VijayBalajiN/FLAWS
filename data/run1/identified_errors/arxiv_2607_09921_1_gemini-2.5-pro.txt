The paper's core claims are based on performance on a held-out test set. Table 1, which provides the summary statistics for the dataset, contains a fundamental mathematical error that invalidates its description.

As defined in the paper, the three deal outcomes ($\SucceedPlus$, $\FailPlus$, $\FailMinus$) are mutually exclusive. Therefore, their percentage distributions must sum to 100%.

However, for the test set, the reported percentages are 84.0%, 3.0%, and 14.4%, which sum to 101.4%. Similarly, for the overall dataset, the percentages are 84.0%, 2.7%, and 13.7%, which sum to 100.4%.

This is a serious flaw. If the basic summary statistics of the dataset are incorrect, the integrity of the entire dataset, the evaluation, and the resulting conclusions are called into question. Such a conspicuous error in the description of the test set, which is the foundation for all of the paper's performance claims, would likely lead a reviewer to distrust the entire study and recommend rejection.
