The paper fundamentally mischaracterizes the properties of the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which is the core of its training objective. This reveals a major conceptual flaw in the paper's central technical argument.

The key error is a contradiction in how the reverse-KL objective is described:
1.  The paper repeatedly claims that its "mode-seeking" reverse-KL objective works by "forcing the student to cover the high-reward modes" and "expands the policy's support" and "expands the student's reasoning-path entropy".
2.  These are the well-known properties of **forward KL** (which is mode-covering or mean-seeking), not reverse KL. Reverse KL is "mode-seeking" or "zero-forcing," which means it encourages the student policy to find and stick to a single high-probability mode of the teacher distribution, typically *reducing* entropy and diversity, not expanding it.
3.  The paper's own ablation study correctly identifies forward KL as "mean-seeking" and "encourages the student to cover all modes," and argues that this is detrimental as it "dilutes focus."

This creates an irreconcilable contradiction: the paper simultaneously argues that mode-covering (a property of forward KL) is undesirable, while claiming that its reverse-KL method is successful precisely because it achieves this very property. This demonstrates a critical misunderstanding of the optimization objective and undermines the entire theoretical justification for the proposed method's success.
