The paper's central claim of being approximately 3.8 times more computationally efficient than the GRPO baseline is founded on misleading and technically unsound FLOPs accounting.

The authors include the computational cost of generating a "rethinking rollout" ($\tilde{y}$) in their total FLOPs budget for SRPO, a step that accounts for over 22% of the method's total cost. However, the paper repeatedly and explicitly states that this rollout is "optional," "not a training target," and used only for "quality validation" and "diagnostic analysis." The costs associated with experimental validation and analysis should not be included in the training cost of the core algorithm, as this misrepresents its true efficiency.

To justify including this cost, the authors make the technically dubious claim of "implementation amortisation," asserting that autoregressively sampling a new trajectory $\tilde{y}$ is "essentially free" because it "shares activations" with the teacher-forcing pass used in Stage 2. This is incorrect. Teacher-forcing involves a single forward pass over the student's trajectory, while autoregressive sampling involves generating a completely new and different trajectory token-by-token. These are distinct computational processes with different inputs after the first token, making significant activation sharing impossible.

By including a costly, non-essential validation step in their primary efficiency metric, the authors are inflating the cost of their method and thereby misrepresenting the efficiency gains compared to baselines. This flawed accounting undermines one of the paper's key contributions.
