:explanation:
The paper's central motivation—that Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are inherently insensitive to low-level artifacts and that these features are "suppressed" in early layers—is directly contradicted by its own analysis.

The authors claim that artifact detection on their $D_2$ dataset is "ineffective throughout" the model layers. However, the caption for the corresponding figure (Figure 3) states that a simple linear probe achieves a validation accuracy that is "stagnant ($\simeq 81\%$) across all depths" for this same dataset. An accuracy of 81% is far from "ineffective"; it indicates that features for detecting low-level artifacts are not only present but are linearly separable at every layer of the model.

This contradiction invalidates the core premise of the paper. The proposed solution, which involves a complex residual injection to bypass early layers, is designed to solve a problem (suppressed or non-existent artifact features) that the paper's own data suggests does not exist. The high linear separability of artifact features implies that the model does not "lack sensitivity" or "overlook" them, undermining the entire justification for the Deep-VRM architecture.
