The paper's central claim is that its performance improvement stems from a novel law-extraction and success-filtering pipeline. To prove this, it presents an ablation study (Table 3) where the filtering mechanism is removed ("w/o Filter"). The paper states this variant suffers a "clear performance decline" due to being trained on noisy data including failures.

However, a direct comparison of the tables reveals a major contradiction. The "w/o Filter" ablation for the Qwen-3-14B model achieves a success rate of 0.71 (Table 3). This is not only a modest drop from the full model's 0.74, but it is also higher than the success rate of *every single baseline* reported in Table 1, including the strongest one, CommPARTNR (0.70).

This contradicts the narrative that the law-guided filtering is the key to outperforming prior work. The results indicate that a simple fine-tuning on all available task data (both successes and failures) is sufficient to beat all baselines. By framing the "w/o Filter" result as a significant failure instead of a very strong baseline in its own right, the paper misattributes the source of its performance gains, which is a critical flaw in the experimental analysis.
