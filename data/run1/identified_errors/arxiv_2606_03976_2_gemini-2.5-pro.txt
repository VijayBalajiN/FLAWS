The paper's primary contribution is to provide a rigorous, information-theoretic formalization of the binding problem. However, the mathematical proofs provided for its core theorems are fundamentally flawed, which severely undermines this central claim.

The most serious error lies in the proof of Theorem 2.8, a result that is crucial for the definition and calculation of conditional binding information used throughout the experiments. The provided proof contains an incorrect algebraic step. Specifically, the equality $H(O, F)-H(O,F\mid Z)=H(F)-H(O\mid Z)$ is false. Given that the feature code $F$ is a deterministic function of the object code $O$, the correct identity is $H(O, F)-H(O,F\mid Z) = H(O)-H(O\mid Z) = I(O;Z)$. The proof incorrectly equates this to $H(F)-H(O\mid Z)$, which would erroneously imply $H(O)=H(F)$. While the theorem statement itself is correct, the flawed proof indicates a critical misunderstanding of basic information theory.

A second, similar error appears in the proof for the Data Processing Inequality (Theorem B.1 in the appendix). The first step of the derivation, "$I(Z_i;O\mid F)=I(Z_i;O\mid F)+I(Z_j; O\mid F, Z_i)$", is logically nonsensical as it equates a quantity $A$ to $A+B$ where $B$ is non-negative.

For a paper that aims to "formalize" a concept, such erroneous proofs for its foundational theorems are a major technical flaw that would likely lead to rejection from a peer-reviewed venue.
