:explanation:
The paper presents a fatal contradiction regarding its methodology for handling different LLM backbones, which undermines its central claim of cross-architecture generalization.

In Section 3.2, the methodology explicitly states that for all cross-architecture results, activations from different models are projected into a shared, low-dimensional space using a trained PCA model. This implies that a single detection framework (prototype) is used in this shared space.

However, Appendix B directly contradicts this, stating emphatically that activations are *not* projected into a shared space and that "Each backbone's detection is self-contained, leveraging only its intrinsic activation geometry." This suggests that a separate, backbone-specific prototype and detection threshold were established for each LLM architecture.

This contradiction makes the generalization experiments in Section 4.3 and Figure 3 irreproducible and their interpretation impossible. It is unclear whether the claimed generalization is inherent to the models' activation spaces (as suggested by the results section), an artifact of a trained projection (as stated in the main methodology), or simply the result of re-calibrating the system for each architecture independently (as implied by the appendix), which would not constitute true generalization. This ambiguity regarding a core component of the proposed method is a critical flaw.
