The paper's core methodological claim is a "failure-driven self-correction" process where agents learn by generating corrective training data from their own *failed* episodes. This is stated explicitly and repeatedly in the introduction and methodology sections.

However, the description of the "\textit{w/o Filter}" ablation study directly contradicts this central claim. It reveals that the full method actually uses a "data filtering step" and that including "failed or law-misaligned episodes introduces noisy or conflicting supervision that negatively impacts learning." This implies that the method's success relies on filtering out failures and training on successful episodes, which is the opposite of the claimed "learn from mistakes" approach.

This contradiction fundamentally misrepresents the paper's contribution. The claimed novelty is learning from failure, but the results suggest that this is detrimental and the actual, undisclosed method involves learning from success. The implementation section further obscures this by mentioning an undefined step, "law-aligned trace $\mathcal{E}_{retain}$ selection," without any explanation, which is likely the critical filtering mechanism. This lack of transparency and direct contradiction between the methodology and the ablation results undermines the validity of the paper's claims.
