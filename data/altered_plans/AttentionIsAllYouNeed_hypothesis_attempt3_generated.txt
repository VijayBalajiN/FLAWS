:original_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model global dependencies between input and output sequences.

:modified_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model global dependencies, including sequence order, between input and output sequences.

:explanation:
The modification introduces the subtle but critical conceptual flaw that attention mechanisms *by themselves* are sufficient to model sequence order. In reality, self-attention mechanisms are permutation-invariant; they treat the input as a set of vectors, not an ordered sequence. Without an external mechanism to provide positional information (like the positional encodings proposed later in the research plan), the model cannot distinguish between "A follows B" and "B follows A". The original hypothesis is correct because the overall *architecture* (which includes positional encodings) is sufficient. The modified hypothesis incorrectly ascribes the property of understanding sequence order to the attention mechanism *alone*, which rests on a false premise about the mechanism's inherent capabilities. This is a sophisticated error because the overall research plan does propose a solution (positional encodings), but the core hypothesis statement misattributes the reason for its potential success.
