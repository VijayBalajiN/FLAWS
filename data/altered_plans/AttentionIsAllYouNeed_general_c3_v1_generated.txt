:original_text:
We will vary the learning rate over the course of training according to the formula: `lr = d_model^-0.5 * min(step_num^-0.5, step_num * warmup_steps^-1.5)`.

:modified_text:
We will vary the learning rate over the course of training according to the formula: `lr = d_model^-0.5 * max(step_num^-0.5, step_num * warmup_steps^-1.5)`.

:explanation:
This error replaces the `min` function with `max`. The original formula relies on `min` to select the smaller of two values, creating a learning rate that first increases linearly (the warm-up) and then switches to an inverse square root decay. By using `max`, the logic is inverted. The schedule will start with the very high initial value of the decay curve (`step_num^-0.5`) and then switch to the linear warm-up curve, which will then increase without bound. This would cause training to diverge, directly falsifying the claim that it is an effective strategy. The mechanism of error is a flaw in the control logic that switches between the schedule's phases.
