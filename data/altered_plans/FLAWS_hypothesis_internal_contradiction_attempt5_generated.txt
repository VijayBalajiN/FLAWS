Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
The criticality of an error in a scientific paper is a function of its connection to the paper's core claims.

:modified_text:
The criticality of an error in a scientific paper is a function of its connection to the paper's core claims, such that the most critical errors are those that remain undetectable even to the sophisticated generative processes that introduce them.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a self-defeating definition of "criticality" that undermines the project's own stated goal of evaluating LLM performance.

The research plan's overall purpose is to create a benchmark to measure how effectively LLMs can find critical errors. The modified hypothesis adds a specific mechanism for defining the "most critical" errors: they are precisely the ones that the error-generating LLM (a sophisticated model itself) cannot find again after creating them.

This creates an internal contradiction. By taking the hypothesis's own stated mechanism to its conclusion, the benchmark is designed to select for errors that are, by definition, undetectable by the very class of state-of-the-art models it aims to evaluate. The goal is to measure capability, but the definition of the most important test items ensures that they represent tasks on which a top-tier model has already failed. This conflates "criticality" with "undetectability by a specific method," creating a test that is inherently biased toward impossibility and thus cannot serve as a fair or informative measure of the models' general error-detection abilities. The machinery for identifying the most important errors guarantees they are beyond the reach of the tools being measured, breaking the evaluation framework.
