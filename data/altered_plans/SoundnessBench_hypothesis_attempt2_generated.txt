Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a bias towards optimism and high sensitivity to prompt framing.

:modified_text:
Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a bias towards optimism that is independent of its high sensitivity to prompt framing.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a subtle conceptual error by claiming the "bias towards optimism" is "independent of" the "high sensitivity to prompt framing." This is a significant flaw because these two phenomena are very likely causally linked, not orthogonal. In language models, a high sensitivity to prompting is the very mechanism through which a baseline bias (like optimism) can be modulated or altered. The hypothesis, as modified, illogically decouples the general bias from the mechanism that governs its expression. It posits a scenario where the model's output tendency (optimism) is a fixed constant, even while the model's behavior is highly malleable by the input prompt. This is theoretically implausible for current LLM architectures and reflects a misunderstanding of how these behavioral characteristics interact. An expert would recognize that if a model is highly sensitive to framing, its biases should also be highly contingent on that framing.
