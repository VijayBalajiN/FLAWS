:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives where each perspective is strictly constrained to evidence from a single, distinct intellectual tradition, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
The hypothesis states that a "robust assessment" is achieved by combining two key mechanisms: (1) "knowledgeable grounding against existing work" (implying a comprehensive review of all relevant literature) and (2) "collective deliberation from diverse perspectives."

The modification introduces an internal contradiction by defining the second mechanism in a way that makes the first one impossible. By specifying that each diverse perspective is "strictly constrained to evidence from a single, distinct intellectual tradition," the hypothesis now mandates that each deliberator be grounded in only a siloed, incomplete subset of the "existing work."

This creates a self-defeating loop: the very method used to ensure diversity simultaneously ensures that the grounding is fragmented and incomplete. A deliberation among agents who are each, by design, only partially informed cannot achieve the comprehensive "knowledgeable grounding" that the hypothesis also claims is essential for a "robust" outcome. The mechanism for diversity directly violates the mechanism for grounding, making the hypothesis's own stated goal unattainable through its own stated process.
