We will compare the performance of `BERT_BASE` and `BERT_LARGE` across all tasks to measure the impact of model scale on downstream performance, especially on smaller datasets.

:modified_text:
We will compare the performance of `BERT_BASE-uncased` and `BERT_LARGE-cased` across all tasks to measure the impact of model scale on downstream performance, especially on smaller datasets.

:explanation:
This modification introduces a critical confounding variable: text casing. The two models not only differ in scale (BASE vs. LARGE) but also in their text normalization (`uncased` vs. `cased`). For many NLP tasks, such as Named Entity Recognition, preserving case is an important signal. Any observed performance difference cannot be cleanly attributed to model scale, as it could be partially or wholly due to the difference in how the models handle capitalization. This invalidates the experiment's design for isolating the effect of scale.

:modified_text:
We will compare the performance of `BERT_BASE` and `BERT_LARGE` across all tasks, holding the learning rate constant at 5e-5 for both models, to measure the impact of model scale on downstream performance, especially on smaller datasets.

:explanation:
This modification introduces a flaw in the experimental fine-tuning procedure. While holding hyperparameters constant seems like a controlled experiment, optimal learning rates are often dependent on model size. A learning rate that is optimal for `BERT_BASE` may be sub-optimal (either too high or too low) for the larger `BERT_LARGE` model, and vice-versa. By not tuning the learning rate separately for each model, the comparison is confounded. The resulting performance difference might reflect an improperly tuned model rather than a true effect of architectural scale.
