:modified_text:
To confirm the contribution of each component, we will conduct several ablation studies, including ... replacing the innovation review board with a single, more powerful LLM-as-a-Judge...

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is a **confounding variable**. The study is supposed to isolate the effect of the review board structure (multiple personas vs. a single entity). By simultaneously changing a second variable—the capability of the underlying model to a "more powerful" one—the experiment becomes confounded. Any observed difference in outcome can no longer be attributed solely to the board structure. For example, if the single judge performs worse, is it because the single-judge setup is inferior, or because the "more powerful" model overthinks or hallucinates more? The results are rendered uninterpretable.

The mechanism of this error is a **misaligned comparison (straw man)**. A single, holistic LLM-as-a-Judge would require a prompt engineered specifically for that role. By instead using a prompt designed for a narrow, adversarial persona ('skeptic'), the single judge is being set up to fail. Its evaluation will be inherently biased and incomplete, not representative of what a properly configured single judge could do. This makes the innovation review board appear superior not because of its intrinsic merit, but because the alternative it's being compared against has been unfairly handicapped.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is **begging the question via a flawed proxy metric**. The experiment's goal is redefined from measuring "benefit" to measuring "feedback diversity." A multi-persona board is, by its very design, guaranteed to produce more diverse feedback than a single LLM. Therefore, the experiment is set up to automatically confirm the board's superiority on this specific metric. However, it fails to establish that "diversity" is a valid proxy for actual evaluation quality or benefit. The experiment proves the board is more diverse, but it doesn't prove it's better, thus failing to support the claim.
