Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:modified_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts, yielding superior performance across both language understanding and unconstrained text generation tasks.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a conflict with the well-established architectural trade-off between autoencoding models (like the proposed BERT) and autoregressive models (like GPT).

(a) The established belief is that there is a fundamental design difference between models optimized for understanding and models optimized for generation. Autoencoding models like BERT, which are pre-trained using a Masked Language Model (MLM) objective, learn powerful representations by considering the full bidirectional context. However, this very mechanism makes them unsuitable for open-ended, unconstrained text generation, which proceeds sequentially (left-to-right) and cannot depend on future, ungenerated tokens. Autoregressive models are specifically designed for this generative task.

(b) The modified hypothesis, while internally consistent (it posits that a more powerful representation should be better at all tasks), directly conflicts with this established belief. It incorrectly claims that the proposed deeply bidirectional architecture will also be superior for "unconstrained text generation." An expert would immediately recognize that the model's core pre-training objective (MLM) is fundamentally incompatible with this task, creating a direct conflict with foundational knowledge about language model architectures.
