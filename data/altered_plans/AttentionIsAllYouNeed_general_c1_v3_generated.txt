:original_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model global dependencies between input and output sequences.

:modified_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model local structural dependencies between input and output sequences.

:explanation:
The flaw is a conceptual substitution of a key term. It replaces "global dependencies" with "local structural dependencies." The primary motivation and key advantage of using attention mechanisms over recurrence is their superior ability to capture long-range, or *global*, dependencies. While attention models *can* capture local information, claiming this is what they are "sufficient" for misses the entire point of the architecture. In fact, convolutional networks are often considered more specialized and efficient for modeling local structure. This change subtly reframes the hypothesis to focus on a secondary capability of the architecture, making the claim significantly weaker and potentially misleading about the research's actual contribution.
