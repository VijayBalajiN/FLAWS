:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic assessment of a research idea that is robust to reviewer subjectivity is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
This modification conflates two distinct concepts of a "good" assessment: (a) an objective assessment that is "robust to reviewer subjectivity," and (b) an assessment that accurately predicts the "consensus of subjective reviewers."

The proposed research method (emulating a board of reviewers) and experiment (using peer review decisions as ground truth) are designed to achieve the latter: modeling the collective, inter-subjective agreement of a human expert community. The modified hypothesis, however, incorrectly claims this process achieves the former: an objective evaluation that transcends or is independent of the very subjectivity it is designed to emulate.

The error is the hidden premise that aggregating multiple subjectivities is the same as eliminating subjectivity. In reality, a process that successfully models a community's consensus is, by definition, aligned with that community's collective biases and preferences, not independent of them. A truly objective assessment might frequently disagree with the human consensus. The hypothesis's reasoning appears sound, but it rests on the flawed assumption that modeling a subjective process is the way to achieve an objective outcome.
