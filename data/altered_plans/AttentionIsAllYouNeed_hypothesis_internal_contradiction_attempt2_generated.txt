Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model global dependencies between input and output sequences.

:modified_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, where attention scores are scaled inversely with the positional distance between tokens to regularize the attention distribution, is sufficient to model global dependencies between input and output sequences.

:explanation:
The hypothesis's stated goal is to sufficiently model "global dependencies," which are relationships between tokens regardless of how far apart they are in a sequence. The core strength of the proposed attention mechanism is that it is inherently distance-agnostic, allowing for direct, constant-time modeling of such long-range interactions.

The modification introduces a new mechanistic rule: attention scores are "scaled inversely with the positional distance." Following this rule to its logical conclusion, the mechanism now explicitly penalizes and weakens the connections between tokens that are far apart. This directly and systematically biases the architecture against learning long-range dependencies. Therefore, the hypothesis's own stated mechanism (distance-penalized attention) actively undermines its own stated goal (modeling global dependencies), creating a self-contained contradiction.
