:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from aligned perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
The mechanism of error is a **mischaracterization of a necessary component**. The flaw substitutes "aligned perspectives" for "diverse perspectives." While "aligned" may sound positive (implying consensus or teamwork), it critically undermines the goal of a "robust" assessment. Robustness requires an idea to be stress-tested against genuinely different and potentially conflicting viewpoints. Deliberation from aligned perspectives is prone to groupthink, reinforcing shared biases rather than identifying hidden flaws. Therefore, this combination of factors is no longer *sufficient* to produce a robust assessment, falsifying the claim.
