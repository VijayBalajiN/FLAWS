:explanation:
The modification introduces a conceptual error by mischaracterizing the LLM's observed "optimism bias" as a "confirmation bias." In cognitive science, confirmation bias is the tendency for an agent to search for, interpret, and recall information in a way that confirms its pre-existing beliefs. Large Language Models do not possess beliefs, intentions, or cognitive states in the human sense. Their tendency toward "optimism" is more accurately understood as a statistical artifact of their training data (which is heavily skewed towards successful, positively-framed research) or a byproduct of alignment techniques like RLHF that encourage agreeable or affirmative responses. Attributing this statistical pattern to a specific psychological mechanism like confirmation bias is an anthropomorphic error. It imposes a causal explanation that is inconsistent with the fundamental architecture of LLMs, which operate on pattern matching and probabilistic text generation, not on belief-driven reasoning.
