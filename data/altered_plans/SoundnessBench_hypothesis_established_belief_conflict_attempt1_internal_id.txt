The primary error, captured in both excerpts, is the unsubstantiated assertion that base models are "more objective." A research hypothesis should propose a testable relationship, not state a conclusion as a premise. Whether base models are more objective is a question the experiment should investigate, not a baseline assumption.

This flaw is serious for two reasons:
1.  **Logical Fallacy:** It engages in begging the question by assuming a key characteristic (objectivity) that is central to the investigation.
2.  **Methodological Invalidity:** It sets up a potentially invalid comparison. Base models are text-completion engines, not instruction-followers. Their failure on this task would likely stem from an inability to adhere to the prompt format, which is different from a reasoned (but biased) assessment. Attributing their potential failure to greater "objectivity" would be a misinterpretation of the model's capabilities and limitations.
