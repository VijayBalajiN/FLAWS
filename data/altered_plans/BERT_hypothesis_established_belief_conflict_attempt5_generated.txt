Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:modified_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers provide a more general and powerful foundation for all language tasks, superseding the capabilities of representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a conflict with the well-established architectural trade-off between autoencoding models (like BERT, which uses bidirectional context) and autoregressive models (like GPT, which uses unidirectional context) in Natural Language Processing.

(a) The established belief is that while bidirectional, autoencoding models are superior for Natural Language Understanding (NLU) tasks (e.g., classification, question answering), they are fundamentally unsuited for coherent, open-ended Natural Language Generation (NLG) tasks. This is because to generate a token, a model can only be conditioned on previously generated tokens (a left-to-right, or unidirectional, context). A model conditioned on the full bidirectional context, including "future" tokens, cannot perform this task without significant modification, as it would create a causal paradox. Autoregressive models are the standard and necessary paradigm for NLG.

(b) The modified hypothesis claims that bidirectional representations provide a foundation that is "more general" and "supersedes" unidirectional models for "all language tasks." This is an overgeneralization that is factually incorrect. The statement is internally consistent—a truly more general model would supersede a less general one—but it clashes with the expert knowledge that the two architectures are suited for different, complementary classes of problems (NLU vs. NLG). An expert would recognize that no single encoder architecture of this type can "supersede" the other across *all* tasks; the choice is dictated by the nature of the task itself.
