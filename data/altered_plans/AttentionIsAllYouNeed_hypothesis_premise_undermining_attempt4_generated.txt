Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to model global dependencies between input and output sequences.

:modified_text:
An architecture relying solely on attention mechanisms, without recurrence or convolutions, is sufficient to capture the complete relational structure, including global dependencies, between input and output sequences.

:explanation:
This modification conflates two distinct concepts: (1) the ability to model **global dependencies** between tokens, and (2) the ability to capture the **complete relational structure** of a sequence.

The "complete relational structure" of a sequence includes not only the dependencies between its elements but also their fundamental order and relative positions. The attention mechanism, by its nature, is permutation-invariant; it treats its input as an unordered set of vectors. Therefore, an architecture relying *solely* on attention is insufficient to capture sequential order. The actual Transformer architecture requires an additional, non-attention-based component (positional encodings) to inject this crucial information.

The modified hypothesis incorrectly implies that the attention mechanism alone is sufficient for this broader task, undermining the premise by attributing a capability of the full system (attention + positional encodings) to just one of its components. The formal reasoning is not self-contradictory, but it rests on a flawed assumption about what "attention" by itself can represent.
