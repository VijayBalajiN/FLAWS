:original_text:
collective deliberation from diverse perspectives

:modified_text:
collective consensus-building from diverse perspectives

:explanation:
The conceptual flaw introduced is the replacement of "deliberation" with "consensus-building." This subtly reframes the goal of the multi-perspective process from a robust exploration of an idea's merits to the achievement of agreement. In the context of scientific evaluation, prioritizing consensus is a significant theoretical error. A truly robust assessment process must be able to surface and preserve fundamental disagreements, as these often highlight an idea's most critical weaknesses, underlying assumptions, or points of contention with existing paradigms. By positing "consensus-building" as a core component of a robust process, the hypothesis mistakenly equates agreement with correctness, creating a vulnerability to groupthink and the suppression of valid, insightful minority viewpoints. The most robust outcome of a review is not necessarily a unified opinion, but a clear articulation of the full spectrum of informed judgments, including any irresolvable conflicts.

rests on a false premise
