Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:modified_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful for all language tasks, including both understanding and generation, than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a conflict with the well-established architectural trade-offs between different types of language models. While the statement remains internally consistent, it makes a universal claim of superiority that is incorrect.

The established belief is that there is a fundamental architectural trade-off between models designed for Natural Language Understanding (NLU) and those for Natural Language Generation (NLG).
- **Unidirectional (autoregressive) models**, like GPT, process text in a strict left-to-right order. This causal structure makes them naturally suited for open-ended text generation, as they predict the next token based on the preceding ones.
- **Deeply bidirectional models**, like the proposed BERT, are "non-causal" as every token's representation is influenced by all other tokens in the sequence. This holistic context makes them exceptionally powerful for NLU tasks (e.g., classification, question answering), but their architecture is fundamentally ill-suited for coherent, open-ended generation.

The modified hypothesis incorrectly claims that the proposed bidirectional model will be more powerful for *all* tasks, explicitly including generation. An expert would recognize that this ignores the fundamental architectural advantage of unidirectional models for generation tasks. The proposed model (an encoder) cannot perform generation in the same native, effective way as a unidirectional model (a decoder). The hypothesis creates a false dilemma: either this new model architecture magically overcomes this core trade-off, or a foundational principle of LM architecture is wrong.
