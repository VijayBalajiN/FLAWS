Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:modified_text:
Language representations that capture a sentence's holistic semantic meaning, by being jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers, are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:explanation:
This modification conflates a technical mechanism with a high-level semantic property.

1.  **Distinct Concept 1 (Technical Mechanism):** "Jointly conditioning on both left and right context" is a specific, well-defined computational process. It means that the algorithm for generating a token's vector representation has access to all other tokens in the sequence, both before and after it.
2.  **Distinct Concept 2 (Semantic Property):** "Capturing a sentence's holistic semantic meaning" is a much broader, more abstract, and ultimately philosophical claim about the representation's quality. It implies a true understanding of the sentence's intent, nuance, and compositional structure.

The modified hypothesis incorrectly presents the technical mechanism (Concept 1) as being equivalent to or sufficient for achieving the semantic property (Concept 2). It smuggles in the unproven premise that this specific type of conditioning *is* how holistic meaning is captured. While the proposed bidirectional model may indeed be "more powerful" on benchmarks, the experiment cannot prove the deeper claim that it has captured "holistic semantic meaning." The model could simply be learning more effective statistical patterns for the pre-training task without achieving genuine semantic understanding. The flaw lies in treating a specific computational architecture as interchangeable with the cognitive outcome it is designed to approximate.
