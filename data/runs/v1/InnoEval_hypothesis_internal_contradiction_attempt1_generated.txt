:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making, where the robustness of the final assessment is directly proportional to the variance among the deliberating perspectives.

:explanation:
The modified hypothesis states that its goal is to achieve a "systematic and robust assessment." However, it then defines the mechanism for achieving this robustness in a self-defeating way. It claims that robustness is "directly proportional to the variance among the deliberating perspectives."

Following this stated mechanism to its logical conclusion, to maximize the robustness of the assessment (the goal), one must maximize the variance among the evaluators. A state of maximum variance implies maximum disagreement, divergence, and instability in the outputs. This is the statistical and conceptual opposite of a "robust" outcome, which is characterized by stability, reliability, and low sensitivity to outliers or changes in composition (i.e., low variance).

Therefore, the very mechanism the hypothesis proposes to achieve robustness (maximizing variance) guarantees that the resulting assessment will be non-robust, creating a contradiction that is internal to the hypothesis's own logic.
