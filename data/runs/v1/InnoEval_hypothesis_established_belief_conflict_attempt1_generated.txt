Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A coherent and complete preference ordering of research ideas, which is robust to the inclusion of irrelevant alternatives, is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a conflict with Arrow's Impossibility Theorem, a foundational result in social choice theory. The theorem proves that when there are three or more distinct options to rank, it is impossible to design a system that aggregates individual preferences into a single coherent group ranking while simultaneously satisfying a set of basic fairness criteria.

The modified hypothesis claims that the proposed process can produce a "coherent and complete preference ordering" (a group ranking) that is "robust to the inclusion of irrelevant alternatives." This latter phrase is a direct reference to one of Arrow's key fairness criteria: the Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA). The hypothesis is now claiming that its method (combining diverse perspectives and multi-criteria evaluation) can achieve what Arrow's theorem mathematically proved to be impossible. While the statement is internally logical and describes a desirable outcome for an evaluation system, an expert in social choice theory or related fields would recognize that this claim cannot be reconciled with this well-established and proven theorem.
