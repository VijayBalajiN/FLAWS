The most serious error is in the main hypothesis, which claims the architecture relies "solely on attention mechanisms." This is a fundamental mischaracterization of the proposed model. The Transformer architecture also contains position-wise feed-forward networks within each of its blocks, and these networks are a critical component essential for the model's success. This error misrepresents the core subject of the investigation.

The second error, found in the research question, is the misleading claim that the model works by using a "constant number of sequential operations." While technically true and the basis for its parallelizability, this phrasing strategically omits the model's major drawback: its computational complexity per layer grows quadratically (O(n^2)) with the sequence length. This is a critical trade-off that is not transparently presented.

The third error is a methodological gap. The plan explicitly states a hypothesis for why the scaling factor (1/√d_k) is used in the attention mechanism. However, the experiment design section fails to propose an ablation study or any other test to validate this specific hypothesis. A complete research plan should include experiments to test its own ancillary hypotheses.
