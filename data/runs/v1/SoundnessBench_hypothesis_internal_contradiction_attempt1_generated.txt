:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a bias towards optimism and high sensitivity to prompt framing.

:modified_text:
Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a persistent bias towards optimism, even as its final judgment remains fully contingent on the positive or negative framing of the prompt.

:explanation:
The modified hypothesis creates a self-contradiction by positing two mutually exclusive conditions as simultaneously true.

1.  It claims a "persistent bias towards optimism." The term "persistent bias" implies a stable, internal tendency within the model that consistently influences its judgments in one direction (optimism).
2.  It also claims the model's "final judgment remains fully contingent on the... framing of the prompt." This means the outcome is determined exclusively by an external factor (the prompt), rendering any internal tendency irrelevant.

The contradiction arises because a judgment cannot be both influenced by a persistent internal bias and, at the same time, be "fully contingent" on external framing. If the judgment is truly fully contingent on the prompt, then a negative prompt must produce a negative judgment, which negates the possibility of a "persistent" optimism bias affecting the final output. The hypothesis's own mechanism of prompt-contingency, when taken to its stated logical extreme ("fully"), invalidates its other claim of a persistent internal bias.
