:modified_text:
We will then calculate the raw percentage agreement between our automated evaluation metric and the human consensus to confirm its alignment with human judgment.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is statistical. It replaces a general (and appropriate) comparison with a specific but flawed statistical method. Raw percentage agreement does not account for agreement that occurs by chance, which can be very high in tasks with few categories (e.g., pass/fail). A high raw agreement score could be misleadingly presented as evidence of "consistency," when a more rigorous metric like Cohen's Kappa (which corrects for chance) would reveal the metric is performing poorly. This undermines the claim by using a weak and potentially deceptive standard of evidence for "consistency."
