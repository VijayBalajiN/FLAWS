Two human annotators will assess (1) the validity of the inserted scientific errors and (2) the correctness of the LLMs' identifications.

:modified_text:
Two human annotators will assess (1) the plausibility and topical relevance of the inserted scientific errors and (2) the correctness of the LLMs' identifications.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is **Goalpost Shifting / Lowering the Bar for Validation**. The criteria for human validation have been subtly weakened from "validity" to "plausibility and topical relevance." An error can be plausible and relevant to the paper's topic without being a scientifically valid or meaningful conceptual flaw. This change allows the pipeline to be deemed successful even if it only generates superficial errors, as the human annotators would correctly confirm that the errors meet the new, weaker criteria. This directly undermines the claim that the pipeline generates errors that are "scientifically valid and meaningful."

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is **Selection Bias**. By explicitly stating that human validation is performed only on the subset of errors that survive the "internal identification" filter, the study introduces a significant bias. This filter is designed to remove errors the generating LLM can easily find. The surviving errors are not necessarily more scientifically valid, but may simply be more convoluted or exploit the specific blind spots of the generating LLM. The human validation would therefore be conducted on a non-representative sample, potentially leading to a significant overestimation of the pipeline's overall ability to generate high-quality errors.
