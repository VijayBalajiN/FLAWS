Confounded design

:original_text:
-   **Tasks:** We will primarily evaluate our model on machine translation tasks. Additionally, we will apply it to English constituency parsing to test its ability to generalize to other structured prediction problems.

:modified_text:
-   **Tasks:** We will primarily evaluate our model on machine translation tasks against a state-of-the-art recurrent model. Additionally, we will apply it to English constituency parsing to test its ability to generalize to other structured prediction problems.

:original_text:
-   **Machine Translation:** We will use the WMT 2014 English-German dataset, consisting of approximately 4.5 million sentence pairs, and the WMT 2014 English-French dataset, which is significantly larger with about 36 million sentences.

:modified_text:
-   **Machine Translation:** To evaluate our Transformer model, we will use the WMT 2014 English-French dataset, which is significantly larger with about 36 million sentences. For the recurrent baseline model, we will use the WMT 2014 English-German dataset, consisting of approximately 4.5 million sentence pairs.

:explanation:
The introduced flaw is a confounded design. The experiment now proposes to compare the new Transformer architecture against a baseline recurrent model to test the hypothesis of its superiority. However, the comparison is confounded by the choice of datasets. The Transformer model is allocated the massive WMT 2014 English-French dataset (36 million sentence pairs), while the baseline recurrent model is trained on the much smaller WMT 2014 English-German dataset (4.5 million sentence pairs). Because data quantity is a critical factor in the performance of neural machine translation models, this design makes it impossible to isolate the effect of the model architecture. Any observed performance difference could be attributed to the vast disparity in training data size rather than the inherent advantages of the attention-only mechanism, thus failing to provide valid support for the hypothesis.
