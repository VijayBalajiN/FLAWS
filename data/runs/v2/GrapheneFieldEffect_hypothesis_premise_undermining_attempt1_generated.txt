:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
that we can induce a significant surface charge and thereby modulate the carrier concentration in the film. We will measure the resulting changes in conductivity to determine key parameters such as carrier mobility.

:modified_text:
that we can induce a significant surface charge and thereby modulate the film's conductivity. The measurement of conductivity as a function of gate voltage will directly test this effect and allow us to determine key parameters such as carrier mobility.

:explanation:
The error conflates two distinct physical quantities: **charge carrier concentration** (the number of charge carriers per unit area/volume) and **electrical conductivity** (the measure of how easily charge flows through the material).

The hypothesis is flawed because an external gate voltage directly modulates the charge carrier concentration (`n`), which is the fundamental effect. Electrical conductivity (`σ`) is a resulting, measurable property that depends on both the carrier concentration (`n`) and the carrier mobility (`μ`), as described by the formula `σ = n * q * μ`. By stating that the gate tunes conductivity directly, the hypothesis incorrectly assumes that conductivity is a perfect, one-to-one proxy for carrier concentration. This is a mistake because carrier mobility (`μ`) is not a constant; it can also change as a function of the carrier concentration, temperature, or material defects. For instance, at high concentrations induced by the gate, increased scattering can lower mobility, meaning conductivity will not increase linearly with concentration. The modified text treats the observable effect (conductivity) as if it were the fundamental quantity being controlled, undermining the premise of what the electric field effect actually does.
