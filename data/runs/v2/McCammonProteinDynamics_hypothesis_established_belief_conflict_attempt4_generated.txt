:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a simple diffusional character, with mean-squared displacement increasing linearly with time across all femtosecond-to-picosecond timescales.

:original_text:

:modified_text:
We hypothesize that the protein interior is not a rigid solid but is more fluid-like, with local atomic motions exhibiting a simple diffusional character, with mean-squared displacement increasing linearly with time across all femtosecond-to-picosecond timescales.

:explanation:
(a) The established belief being conflicted with is the well-understood nature of atomic motion in a condensed phase at very short timescales. It is a fundamental result from statistical mechanics and kinetic theory that for any particle in a liquid, solid, or crowded environment like a protein interior, motion over infinitesimally short time intervals is "ballistic," not "diffusional." During this ballistic regime (typically femtoseconds), the particle moves at a near-constant velocity until it collides with a neighbor. Consequently, its mean-squared displacement (MSD) increases with the square of time (MSD ∝ t²). True diffusional behavior, where MSD increases linearly with time (MSD ∝ t), only emerges at longer timescales after the particle has undergone many collisions and its motion has been randomized.

(b) The modified hypothesis is internally consistent but clashes with this established belief. It incorrectly posits that the motion has a "simple diffusional character" and that the MSD increases linearly with time "across all femtosecond-to-picosecond timescales." This claim is physically impossible. An expert would immediately recognize that the MSD *must* be quadratic (ballistic) in the femtosecond regime and cannot be linear. The hypothesis mistakes the long-time characteristic of diffusion for a universal behavior applicable at all timescales, a subtle but significant error in physical reasoning. The proposed experiment would, in fact, reveal a quadratic relationship at short times, directly falsifying the modified hypothesis.
