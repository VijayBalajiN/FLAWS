The modification changes the fundamental description of graphene from a "semimetal" to a "direct-gap semiconductor." This conflicts with the well-established, Nobel Prize-winning discovery that intrinsic single-layer graphene is a zero-bandgap semimetal, not a semiconductor with a finite energy gap. Its unique electronic properties, including its extremely high carrier mobility and characteristic ambipolar field effect, are direct consequences of its semimetallic nature and linear band dispersion at the Dirac points.

The modified hypothesis is internally consistent: a two-dimensional direct-gap semiconductor could plausibly exhibit an ambipolar field effect, and the research plan to fabricate and test such a material is logical. The error is that graphene is not this material.
