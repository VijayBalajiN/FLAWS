:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character, yet are governed by strong, harmonic restorative forces that effectively return each atom to its mean crystallographic position.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a direct internal contradiction between the type of atomic motion claimed and the physical mechanism governing it.

The first part of the hypothesis claims that atomic motions have a "diffusional character." In physics, diffusion describes a random walk process where a particle's mean-square displacement from its starting point grows over time; it does not have a tendency to return to its origin.

The second, modified part of the hypothesis states these same motions are "governed by strong, harmonic restorative forces that effectively return each atom to its mean crystallographic position." A harmonic restorative force (like a spring, F = -kx) creates oscillatory, not diffusional, motion. An atom under such a force will vibrate around a fixed equilibrium point, and its time-averaged position will remain constant. This is the physical definition of non-diffusional, localized motion.

Therefore, the hypothesis's own stated mechanism (harmonic restorative forces) makes its own central claim (diffusional character) physically impossible. The two concepts are mutually exclusive, creating a self-contained contradiction.
