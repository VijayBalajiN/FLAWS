:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where the diffusional character of local atomic motions is directly proportional to their root-mean-square fluctuation.

:explanation:
The modification conflates two distinct physical concepts: "diffusional character" and "root-mean-square fluctuation" (RMSF).

1.  **Diffusional Character:** This is a *dynamic* property that describes the time-dependent nature of motion. True diffusion follows a random walk, where the mean-square displacement of a particle increases linearly with time. It is characterized by a diffusion coefficient, which measures the rate of this random exploration of space.

2.  **Root-Mean-Square Fluctuation (RMSF):** This is an *equilibrium*, time-averaged property that measures the magnitude or amplitude of an atom's movement around its average position. It describes the size of the region an atom occupies but says nothing about the nature or timescale of the motion within that region.

The error is in positing a direct proportionality between these two. An atom can be confined to a wide, but steep, potential well. It would have a large RMSF (high magnitude of fluctuation) but would exhibit purely oscillatory, non-diffusional motion. Conversely, an atom could be confined to a very small region (small RMSF) but still move with a purely diffusional character within that space. The hypothesis smuggles in the flawed premise that the size of an atom's "cage" (RMSF) is the same as the rate at which it randomly moves, which is a non-trivial and incorrect simplification of complex dynamics.
