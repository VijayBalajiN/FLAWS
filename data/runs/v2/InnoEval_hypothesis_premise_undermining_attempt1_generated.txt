:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea that reaches human-expert-level accuracy is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
This modification conflates two distinct concepts: (1) a truly "systematic and robust assessment," which implies an intrinsically sound and objective evaluation of a research idea's merit, and (2) an assessment that achieves "human-expert-level accuracy," which, given the experiment design, means successfully predicting the outcomes of the existing, potentially flawed human peer-review process.

The error is a subtle but critical undermining of the premise. The research plan's "Problem" section explicitly states that the current human evaluation process is "inherently subjective." By modifying the hypothesis to define its goal as achieving "accuracy" against this very benchmark, it implicitly assumes that replicating a flawed process is equivalent to achieving a robust one. A truly robust system might be valuable precisely when it *disagrees* with the subjective consensus of human experts. The hypothesis now equates the ideal goal (robustness) with the flawed proxy used for measurement (predictive accuracy), a common but significant mistake in building evaluation systems.
