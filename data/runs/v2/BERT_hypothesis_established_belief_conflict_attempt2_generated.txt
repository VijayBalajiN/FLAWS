Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts.

:modified_text:
Language representations jointly conditioned on both left and right context in all layers are more powerful than representations conditioned on unidirectional or shallowly combined bidirectional contexts, enabling superior performance in both understanding and standard autoregressive generation tasks.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a conflict with a fundamental, well-established principle of Transformer architectures.

(a) The established belief is that there is an architectural trade-off between natural language understanding (NLU) and natural language generation (NLG). Standard autoregressive generation (e.g., in models like GPT) requires a causal attention mask, where each token can only attend to previous tokens to predict the next one. The proposed model (BERT) is a deep bidirectional encoder where every token attends to every other token (both past and future). This non-causal structure is what makes it powerful for NLU tasks but architecturally incapable of performing standard, left-to-right autoregressive generation.

(b) The modified hypothesis claims that this single, deeply bidirectional architecture will be superior for both understanding tasks and "standard autoregressive generation tasks." This is a direct conflict with the established principle. While the hypothesis is internally consistent (it simply makes a bold, unified claim), an expert would recognize that the proposed architecture cannot, by definition, perform the generative task described. It would require a completely different attention mechanism (a causal mask) or a separate decoder component, neither of which is part of the proposed model. The claim creates a fork: either the hypothesis is wrong, or a fundamental principle of Transformer mechanics is wrong.
