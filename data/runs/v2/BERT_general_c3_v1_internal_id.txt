:explanation:
The most serious error is that the ablation study designed to measure the effect of model size is fundamentally confounded. By pre-training `BERT_BASE` and `BERT_LARGE` on different datasets, any observed performance difference cannot be attributed solely to model scale. The larger and more diverse dataset for `BERT_LARGE` introduces a confounding variable, making it impossible to isolate the impact of the model's architecture and size. To be a valid comparison, both models should have been trained on the exact same corpus.

The second error is a methodological flaw in the feature-based ablation study. The goal is to compare the fine-tuning approach to a feature-based one. However, by feeding the extracted features into a BiLSTM classifier, the experiment introduces another powerful bidirectional model at the task-specific level. This confounds the comparison, as it's unclear whether good performance comes from the pre-trained BERT features or the task-specific BiLSTM. A cleaner experiment would use a simpler, non-bidirectional classifier to evaluate the power of the fixed BERT representations themselves.
