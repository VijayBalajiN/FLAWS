:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character, constrained by strong harmonic restoring forces that maintain the global X-ray crystallographic structure.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a mechanism that contradicts the hypothesis's own stated outcome. The hypothesis claims the protein interior is "fluid-like" and that atomic motions are "diffusional." However, the added mechanism states these motions are "constrained by strong harmonic restoring forces."

By definition, a system governed by strong harmonic restoring forces is a harmonic solid, where particles oscillate around fixed equilibrium positions but do not diffuse. Diffusional motion, characteristic of a fluid, requires particles to be able to move freely from their initial positions, leading to a mean-squared displacement that grows over time.

Thus, the proposed mechanism (harmonic forces, implying a solid-like state) makes the claimed outcome (diffusional motion, implying a fluid-like state) physically impossible. The hypothesis's own machinery, when taken to its logical conclusion, invalidates its own central claim.
