:explanation:
(a) The established belief being conflicted with is the fundamental ambipolar nature of electronic transport in graphene. This property, which has been extensively verified experimentally and theoretically, arises directly from graphene's unique electronic band structure: its symmetric, linear dispersion relation where the conduction and valence bands meet at the Dirac points. This symmetry dictates that both electrons and holes can be induced with a gate voltage and exhibit similar transport properties.

(b) The modified hypothesis is internally consistent; it proposes a unipolar field effect, a common phenomenon in many materials where only one type of charge carrier (e.g., electrons) dominates transport. However, this claim directly contradicts the established electron-hole symmetry of graphene. An expert would immediately recognize that proposing a fundamentally unipolar field effect for intrinsic graphene is a deep misconception about the material's core electronic properties. The hypothesis is plausible for a generic, unknown 2D material but is irreconcilable with the known physics of graphene.
