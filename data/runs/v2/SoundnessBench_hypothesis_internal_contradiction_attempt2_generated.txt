:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a bias towards optimism and high sensitivity to prompt framing.

:modified_text:
Large Language Models' assessment of a research proposal's methodological soundness is unreliable, exhibiting a bias towards optimism that is exacerbated by a high sensitivity to prompt framing, which consistently amplifies the model's default evaluative stance regardless of the frame's valence.

:explanation:
The modified hypothesis contains an internal contradiction between its central claim ("high sensitivity to prompt framing") and the mechanism it proposes to explain that sensitivity.

1.  **The Claim:** The hypothesis states that LLMs exhibit "high sensitivity to prompt framing." In the context of evaluation, this means the model's judgment can be swayed in different directions based on the framing of the prompt (e.g., a neutral prompt yields one result, while a critical/aggressive prompt yields a more negative one).

2.  **The Proposed Mechanism:** The hypothesis then defines the mechanism of this sensitivity: it "consistently amplifies the model's default evaluative stance [optimism] regardless of the frame's valence."

3.  **The Contradiction:** If the mechanism for "sensitivity" is to *always* amplify the default optimistic bias, then the model is, by definition, *not* sensitive to the prompt's framing or valence. A critical prompt designed to induce skepticism would, according to this mechanism, instead make the model *more* optimistic. The model's output would not change direction based on the prompt's intent; it would only change in magnitude along a single, positive direction. This directly contradicts the meaning of "high sensitivity to prompt framing," which requires the model's behavior to be alterable in different directions by the frame. The hypothesis's own stated mechanism makes the phenomenon it claims to describe impossible.
