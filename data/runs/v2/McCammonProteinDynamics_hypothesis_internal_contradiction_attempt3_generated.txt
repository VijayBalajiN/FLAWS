:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character, driven by thermal fluctuations within the local harmonic potential wells that define the protein's equilibrium X-ray structure.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a mechanism that contradicts the hypothesis's primary claim. The hypothesis states that the protein interior is "fluid-like" and motions are "diffusional." However, the added clause specifies that these motions are "driven by thermal fluctuations within the local harmonic potential wells that define the protein's equilibrium X-ray structure."

A harmonic potential well implies a restoring force that is proportional to the displacement from a fixed equilibrium position (like a mass on a spring). Motion governed by such a potential is, by physical definition, oscillatory, not diffusional. This is the characteristic behavior of atoms in a solid crystal lattice. In contrast, diffusional motion (like a random walk in a fluid) is characterized by the absence of a persistent restoring force pulling the particle back to a single origin point; the particle is free to wander away.

Therefore, the stated mechanism (motion within harmonic wells) directly generates behavior (oscillation around a fixed point) that is the antithesis of the claimed outcome (diffusional motion in a fluid-like environment). The hypothesis's own physical model invalidates its central claim.
