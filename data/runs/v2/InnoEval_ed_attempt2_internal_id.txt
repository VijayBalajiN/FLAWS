The experiment's core validity is undermined by a fundamental flaw in its ground-truth assumption. The design conflates the peer-review outcome of a full research *paper* (e.g., Accept/Reject) with the intrinsic quality of the underlying research *idea*. A paper's final decision is influenced by many factors beyond the idea itself, including the quality of execution, experimental results, writing clarity, and presentation.

The framework is designed to assess nascent ideas, but the experiment evaluates its ability to predict the success of fully executed and documented research papers. This is a different and potentially easier task. Furthermore, extracting an "idea" from a completed paper risks data leakage, as the structured format may implicitly contain information about the successful execution and results, which would not be available for a genuine, unevaluated idea. This mismatch between the stated problem (evaluating ideas) and the experimental task (predicting paper outcomes) invalidates the proposed method of validation.
