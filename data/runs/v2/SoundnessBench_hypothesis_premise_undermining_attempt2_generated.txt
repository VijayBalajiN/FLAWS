Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
This approach uses expert-derived scores as a proxy for methodological validity.

:modified_text:
This approach uses expert-derived scores to establish the ground-truth labels for methodological validity.

:explanation:
The modification conflates two distinct concepts: (a) **objective methodological soundness**, which is the idealized, ground-truth quality of a research plan, and (b) **peer-review consensus**, which is the aggregated opinion of a small set of human experts and is used here as a measurement.

The original text correctly identifies the peer review scores as a "proxy" for validity, acknowledging the potential gap between the measurement and the true concept. The modified text eliminates this critical nuance. By changing the hypothesis to frame "divergence from expert peer-review consensus" as a primary indicator of unreliability, and by explicitly relabeling the proxy as the "ground-truth," the plan now silently assumes that the reviewers' collective judgment *is* objective soundness.

This is a mistake because peer review is a fallible human process with known biases (e.g., towards incrementalism, against high-risk novelty) and noise. A truly sound but unconventional proposal could be rejected by reviewers, while a flawed but trendy one could be accepted. Under the modified hypothesis's flawed premise, an LLM that correctly identified a flaw that human reviewers missed would be incorrectly penalized for "diverging from the consensus," undermining the entire goal of evaluating the model's capacity for objective scientific assessment.
