:explanation:
The modification replaces "semimetals" with "direct-gap semiconductors". The resulting hypothesis is internally consistent: a two-dimensional direct-gap semiconductor could plausibly exhibit a strong, ambipolar electric field effect. The statement does not contradict itself.

However, this modified hypothesis directly conflicts with one of the most fundamental and well-established results in condensed matter physics: that monolayer graphene (the "atomically thin graphitic film" in question) is a zero-gap semimetal. Its unique electronic properties, including the behavior of its charge carriers as massless Dirac fermions, stem from its gapless, linear band structure around the Dirac points. Claiming it is a direct-gap semiconductor—a material with a finite energy gap between its valence and conduction bands—is a fundamental error that contradicts decades of theoretical and experimental research, including the work that won the 2010 Nobel Prize in Physics. An expert would immediately recognize this conflict, while a non-specialist might see "semiconductor" and find the claim perfectly reasonable in the context of electronics research.
