:modified_text:
We will source our papers from the accepted list of the 2025 International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), ensuring their final versions were first posted on arXiv after the knowledge cutoff dates of the models we plan to test.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is an incomplete version history check. The addition of the phrase "final versions" creates a subtle but critical loophole. A paper's initial preprint (e.g., v1) could have been posted on arXiv *before* the model's knowledge cutoff, making it part of the training data. The "final version" (the camera-ready copy for the conference) might be posted after the cutoff, but the model would have already been exposed to the paper's core content, text, and structure. This would contaminate the benchmark, invalidating the claim that the method reliably prevents it.

:modified_text:
We will source our papers from the accepted list of the 2025 International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), ensuring they were first posted on arXiv after the publicly stated pre-training knowledge cutoff dates of the models we plan to test.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is a misunderstanding of how model knowledge cutoffs work. Specifying "publicly stated pre-training" knowledge cutoffs sounds rigorous but is misleading. State-of-the-art models often undergo continuous fine-tuning or reinforcement learning (RLHF) on data much newer than their base pre-training corpus. The model being evaluated via an API may have been updated with data that post-dates the pre-training cutoff, including the very papers selected for the benchmark. This falsifies the claim by failing to account for the model's full, evolving training data history.

:original_text:
We will source our papers from the accepted list of the 2025 International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), ensuring they were first posted on arXiv after the knowledge cutoff dates of the models we plan to test.

:modified_text:
We will source our papers from the accepted list of the 2025 International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML). To prevent data contamination, our sole inclusion criterion will be that the papers were first posted on arXiv after the knowledge cutoff dates of the models we plan to test.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is conflating direct textual contamination with indirect conceptual contamination. By stating that the arXiv date is the "sole inclusion criterion," the method explicitly ignores the paper's intellectual lineage. A paper from 2025 could be a direct, incremental follow-up to a highly influential 2024 paper that the model has memorized. The model might "know" the methodology, dataset, and likely conclusions of the 2025 paper without ever having seen its literal text. This indirect contamination undermines the benchmark's validity, as the model isn't reasoning from scratch. The claim of reliable contamination prevention is false because it only considers the most simplistic form of contamination.
