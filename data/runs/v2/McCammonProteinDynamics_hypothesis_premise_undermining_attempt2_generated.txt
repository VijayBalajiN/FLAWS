:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment whose diffusional character is established by the large amplitude of its local atomic motions.

:explanation:
The modified hypothesis conflates two distinct physical concepts: (a) the **amplitude of atomic motion** and (b) the **diffusional character of that motion**.

1.  **Amplitude of motion** refers to the spatial magnitude of atomic fluctuations, often quantified by the root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF). It describes *how far* atoms move from their average positions.
2.  **Diffusional character** is a time-dependent property describing the nature of the motion. It implies that an atom's mean-squared displacement (MSD) grows linearly with time, indicating that the atom is not confined to a single location but can explore its environment, much like a particle in a liquid.

The error lies in the premise that a large amplitude is sufficient to establish a diffusional character. An atom can exhibit large-amplitude motion while being harmonically confined within a single potential energy well (i.e., "rattling in a cage"). This motion is oscillatory, not diffusional, and its MSD would plateau over time. Conversely, true diffusion could occur with a small amplitude if an atom hops between very closely spaced energy minima. The modified hypothesis incorrectly treats a measure of spatial extent as being definitionally equivalent to a specific dynamic regime, a subtle but critical flaw in its physical reasoning.
