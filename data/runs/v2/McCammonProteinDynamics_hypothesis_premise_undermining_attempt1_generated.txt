:error_type:
Undermining a Premise

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where the diffusional character of local atomic motions is defined by the magnitude of their root-mean-square fluctuations.

:explanation:
The modification conflates two distinct physical concepts: (1) the "diffusional character" of atomic motion and (2) the "magnitude of root-mean-square fluctuations" (RMSF).

1.  **Diffusional Character:** This is a dynamic property describing the *time-dependence* of an atom's movement. A motion is diffusive if the atom undergoes a random walk, such that its mean square displacement from its starting point grows linearly with time. This implies the atom is not confined to a single potential well.
2.  **Magnitude of RMSF:** This is a time-averaged, equilibrium property that measures the *amplitude* of an atom's vibration around its average position. It quantifies how much an atom "jiggles" in place.

The error lies in defining one in terms of the other. An atom can have a very large RMSF (high-amplitude jiggling) while being completely confined within a harmonic potential well; this motion is vibrational, not diffusive. Conversely, true diffusion describes the ability of the atom to escape that local confinement. The modified hypothesis incorrectly assumes that a large amplitude of motion is, by definition, diffusive motion. This is a subtle but fundamental error in statistical mechanics that conflates the size of thermal fluctuations with their specific temporal nature.
