Stable, atomically thin graphitic films are two-dimensional semimetals that exhibit a strong, ambipolar electric field effect, allowing the concentration of their charge carriers—both electrons and holes—to be tuned by an external gate voltage.

:modified_text:
Stable, atomically thin polycrystalline graphitic films are two-dimensional semimetals that exhibit a strong, ambipolar electric field effect, allowing the concentration of their charge carriers—both electrons and holes—to be tuned by an external gate voltage.

:explanation:
The mechanism of this error is the introduction of a flawed material premise. By specifying "polycrystalline," the text now describes a film composed of many small crystal domains. While each individual domain is a semimetal, the macroscopic electronic transport properties of the film will be dominated by scattering at the grain boundaries between these domains. This scattering disrupts coherent charge transport, meaning the film as a whole will not exhibit the clean, fundamental electronic properties of an ideal two-dimensional semimetal. The claim is undermined because the specific material described is known to not behave as a pristine semimetal on a device-relevant scale.
