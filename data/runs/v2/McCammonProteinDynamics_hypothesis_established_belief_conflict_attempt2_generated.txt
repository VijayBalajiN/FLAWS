Conflict with Established Beliefs

:original_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit a diffusional character.

:modified_text:
The interior of a folded globular protein is a fluid-like environment where local atomic motions exhibit an unconstrained, isotropic diffusional character, similar to that of a simple fluid.

:explanation:
(a) The established belief being conflicted with is the core principle of protein structural dynamics that the protein interior is an extremely crowded, anisotropic, and constrained environment. The packing density of atoms in a protein core is comparable to that of a solid organic crystal. Consequently, atomic motions are highly constrained by covalent bonds and steric barriers, and they are strongly anisotropic, meaning they are channeled along specific, low-energy pathways rather than being free to move equally in all directions.

(b) The modified hypothesis, while internally consistent, directly contradicts this established understanding. By claiming that atomic motions have an "unconstrained, isotropic diffusional character, similar to that of a simple fluid," it posits a physical model that is fundamentally incorrect for a protein. Unlike atoms in a simple fluid (like liquid argon), which experience relatively unconstrained and direction-independent (isotropic) diffusion, atoms in a protein core are trapped in local potential energy wells and their movements are severely restricted and directional. An expert would immediately recognize that this assumption ignores the fundamental role of dense packing and the complex, rugged energy landscape in dictating protein dynamics.
