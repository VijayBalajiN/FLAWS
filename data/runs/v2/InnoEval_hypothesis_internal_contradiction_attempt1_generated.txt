:error_type:
Internal Contradiction

:original_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:modified_text:
A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives where each perspective is independently grounded in a disjoint subset of evidence to ensure maximal cognitive independence, and multi-criteria decision-making.

:explanation:
The modification introduces a self-contradiction by specifying the mechanism for achieving "diverse perspectives" in a way that undermines both "collective deliberation" and the overall goal of a "systematic and robust assessment."

The hypothesis now states that diverse perspectives are ensured by having each perspective grounded in a "disjoint subset of evidence." By definition, disjoint sets have no elements in common. If each reviewer persona operates on a completely separate, non-overlapping body of evidence, there is no shared factual basis upon which a "collective deliberation" can occur. The reviewers would be arguing from different realities, making any synthesis of their views incoherent.

This mechanism, followed to its logical conclusion, directly violates the hypothesis's own stated components and goals:
1.  It makes "collective deliberation" impossible, as there is no common ground to deliberate upon.
2.  It prevents the final assessment from being "systematic" and "robust," as a truly systematic assessment requires an integrated and coherent synthesis of evidence, which is impossible when the evidence itself is forcibly fragmented and segregated among the evaluators.

The mechanism designed to ensure independence thus breaks the machinery of deliberation and makes the stated goal of a systematic assessment logically unattainable.
