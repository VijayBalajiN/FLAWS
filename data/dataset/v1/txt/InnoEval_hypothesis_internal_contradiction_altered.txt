## Problem

The proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has dramatically accelerated the generation of scientific ideas, yet our ability to evaluate these ideas has not kept pace. This creates a significant bottleneck at the beginning of the research pipeline, as current evaluation methods rely on human experts, a process that is slow, costly, and inherently subjective.

An ideal scientific evaluation process should be defined by three core principles: knowledgeable grounding against the existing body of work, collective deliberation among diverse experts, and multi-criteria decision-making across various attributes like novelty and feasibility. However, existing automated evaluation tools fall short on all three fronts. They suffer from a narrow knowledge horizon, typically limited to static academic papers; they rely on a single LLM-as-a-Judge, which entrenches model bias and fails to simulate expert consensus; and they flatten the evaluation into one or two dimensions, providing little actionable feedback.

To address these shortcomings, we propose to reframe idea evaluation as a knowledge-grounded, multi-perspective reasoning problem. Our central hypothesis is that by emulating the holistic process of human scholarly review—from evidence gathering to multi-faceted deliberation—we can develop an automated framework capable of providing systematic, robust, and human-expert-level assessments of research ideas.

## Hypothesis

A systematic and robust assessment of a research idea is achieved through a process that combines knowledgeable grounding against existing work, collective deliberation from diverse perspectives where each perspective is strictly constrained to evidence from a single, distinct intellectual tradition, and multi-criteria decision-making.

## Method

We will develop InnoEval, a deep innovation evaluation framework designed to model the scholarly review process. Our approach is structured into four main stages:

1.  **Heterogeneous Deep Knowledge Search:** To overcome the narrowness of existing knowledge sources, we will design a search engine that retrieves dynamic, "living knowledge" from diverse online sources, including scholarly literature (e.g., arXiv, Google Scholar), web content (e.g., Google Search), and code repositories (e.g., GitHub). This engine will employ an iterative search-and-refine strategy, using a hybrid of fast API-based retrieval and slow, in-depth content analysis (e.g., reading full PDFs, analyzing code). A hybrid scoring function combining semantic similarity and LLM-based judgment will be used to rank and filter the most relevant evidence.

2.  **Knowledge Grounding:** To ensure the evaluation is well-founded, a grounding agent will meticulously link the retrieved knowledge to specific components of the research idea. This agent will distill supporting or contradictory evidence and provide a detailed analysis of its relevance, creating a fine-grained evidential base for the subsequent evaluation.

3.  **Multi-dimensional, Multi-perspective Evaluation:** To simulate collective deliberation and avoid the pitfalls of a single LLM-as-a-Judge, we will implement two key features:
    *   **Innovation Review Board:** We will curate a pool of academic personas, each with a distinct background, expertise, and reviewing preference. For each idea, we will sample a small board of these personas to act as reviewers. To simulate realistic cognitive diversity, we will partially mask the retrieved knowledge based on each persona's defined familiarity with different knowledge sources.
    *   **Multi-dimensional Decoupled Evaluation:** We will assess each idea across five independent dimensions: Clarity, Novelty, Feasibility, Validity, and Significance. Each dimension will be evaluated by a specialized agent, guided by the grounded knowledge and the perspective of its assigned persona.

4.  **Report Generation:** Finally, a report agent will synthesize all evaluations into a comprehensive and actionable report. For a single idea, this will include the grounded knowledge, structured multi-dimensional scores and analyses, a meta-review with a final decision, and concrete suggestions for improvement. For a group of ideas, the agent will also provide a comparative analysis and a final ranking.

## Experiment Design

To validate our InnoEval framework, we will conduct a series of experiments designed to assess its performance on point-wise, pair-wise, and group-wise evaluation tasks.

**1. Dataset Construction:**
We will create new benchmark datasets derived from real-world, peer-reviewed research.
*   **Point-wise Dataset:** We will crawl papers from top-tier conferences (NeurIPS and ICLR) via OpenReview and perform stratified sampling based on their final decisions (Reject, Poster, Spotlight, Oral). We will use an extraction agent to convert these papers into a structured idea format, followed by manual verification.
*   **Group-wise and Pair-wise Datasets:** Using the point-wise dataset as a seed, we will retrieve topically similar papers for each idea, selecting one from each decision category to form a group. This process will yield groups of ideas with an inherent ground-truth ranking, from which we can also derive pair-wise comparison tasks.

**2. Evaluation Tasks and Baselines:**
We will evaluate InnoEval against a comprehensive set of baselines, including naive methods (CoT, RAG), idea generation methods with review components, end-to-end scientific discovery frameworks, and other specialized idea evaluation methods.
*   **Point-wise Tasks:** We will frame this as a classification problem (binary: Accept/Reject; ternary: Reject/Poster/Highlight) and measure performance using Accuracy and macro F1 score.
*   **Group-wise Tasks:** We will evaluate the ability to select the best idea in a group (measured by Accuracy) and to produce a full ranking (measured by Accuracy and Longest Increasing Subsequence score).

**3. Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
To confirm the contribution of each component, we will conduct several ablation studies, including removing the knowledge grounding module, replacing the innovation review board with a single LLM-as-a-Judge, and restricting the knowledge search to only academic literature. We will also perform deeper analyses to explore the framework's behavior, such as examining performance scaling with the number of reviewer personas, evaluating the quality of our search engine on metrics like relevance and diversity, and analyzing the inter-correlations between the evaluation dimensions to see if they align with human cognitive patterns.
